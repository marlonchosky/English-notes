# Distributive and Dual-Domain Quantifiers: Syntax and Bounded Domains

In English syntax, **Distributive Quantifiers** and **Dual-Domain Quantifiers** occupy a distinct operational space between indefinite quantifiers and universal operators. Rather than evaluating a set as a collective whole or an open-ended mass (*all*, *some*), they force the syntax to evaluate entities individually or restrict quantification to a strictly bounded domain.

---

## 1. Master Taxonomy: Domain Size and Evaluation Modes

Quantifiers in this category are defined by two core structural parameters: **Group Size (Domain)** and **Evaluation Mode** (Collective vs. Distributive).

| Quantifier  | Domain Size ($N$) | Evaluation Mode            | Polarity / Value            | Verb Agreement | Example Construction             |
| :---------- | :---------------- | :------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :------------: | :------------------------------- |
| **Both**    | Exactly 2 ($N=2$) | **Dual-Collective**        | Positive (+2)               |   **Plural**   | *"**Both** systems are online."* |
| **Either**  | Exactly 2 ($N=2$) | **Dual-Distributive**      | Disjunctive Choice (1 of 2) |  **Singular**  | *"**Either** path is valid."*    |
| **Neither** | Exactly 2 ($N=2$) | **Dual-Distributive**      | Negative Universal (0 of 2) |  **Singular**  | *"**Neither** option worked."*   |
| **Each**    | $N \ge 2$         | **Pure Distributive**      | Individual (1 by 1)         |  **Singular**  | *"**Each** node has a key."*     |
| **Every**   | $N \ge 3$         | **Aggregate Distributive** | Total Individualized (100%) |  **Singular**  | *"**Every** server was tested."* |

---

## 2. Dual-Domain Quantifiers: The Rule of Two (*Both*, *Either*, *Neither*)

Dual-domain operators strictly require an antecedent or contextual domain of **exactly two entities ($N=2$)**. Applying them to domains of three or more triggers an immediate semantic and syntactic crash.

### A. Both (Universal Positive Dual)
* **Domain:** Exactly $N=2$.
* **Evaluation Mode:** **Dual-Collective** (Evaluates the two entities together as a combined pair, forcing plural concord).
* **Syntactic Distribution:** Projects as a **Pre-Determiner**, **Central Determiner ($D^0$)**, or **Independent Pronoun Head ($NP/DP$)**.
  * *Central Determiner:* *"**Both** servers are responsive."*
  * *Pre-Determiner:* *"**Both** the servers are responsive."*
  * *Pronoun / Partitive:* *"**Both of the** servers are responsive."* / *"Two nodes crashed, and **both** failed silently."*

### B. Either (Distributive Choice Dual)
* **Domain:** Exactly $N=2$.
* **Evaluation Mode:** **Dual-Distributive Disjunctive** (Evaluates Item A *or* Item B).
* **Syntactic Distribution:** Functions as a **Determiner ($D^0$)** or **Pronoun Head ($NP/DP$)**.
* **Agreement:** Mandates **Singular Verb Agreement**.
  * *Determiner:* *"You can route traffic through **either** gateway."*
  * *Pronoun / Partitive:* *"**Either of the** gateways is acceptable."* / *"I tested two routes; I cannot recommend **either**."*

### C. Neither (Universal Negative Dual)
* **Domain:** Exactly $N=2$.
* **Evaluation Mode:** **Dual-Distributive Negative** (Evaluates Not A *and* Not B: $0/2$).
* **Syntactic Distribution:** Functions as a **Determiner ($D^0$)** or **Pronoun Head ($NP/DP$)**.
* **Agreement:** Mandates **Singular Verb Agreement**.
  * *Determiner:* *"**Neither** database replica responded to the ping."*
  * *Pronoun / Partitive:* *"**Neither of the** database replicas was accessible."*

## 3. Pure Distributives vs. Aggregate Distributives (*Each* vs. *Every*)

Distributive operators force syntax to process entities individually rather than as an unpartitioned group.

### A. Each (Pure Distributive)
* **Domain:** $N \ge 2$ (Applicable to pairs and larger groups).
* **Evaluation Mode:** **Pure Distributive** (Evaluates items individually in complete isolation).
* **Developer Analogy:** A `forEach()` loop executing logic on one element at a time in isolation.
* **Syntactic Behavior:** Functions as both a **Determiner ($D^0$)** and an **Independent Pronoun Head ($NP/DP$)**.
  * *Determiner:* *"**Each** developer submitted their code review."*
  * *Pronoun / Partitive:* *"**Each of the** developers submitted their code review."*
  * *Bare Elliptical Pronoun:* *"Three deployment plans were proposed; **each** has distinct trade-offs."*

### B. Every (Aggregate Distributive)
* **Domain:** $N \ge 3$ (Canonical minimum threshold for aggregation; rejected for strict dual sets).
* **Evaluation Mode:** **Aggregate Distributive** (Evaluates entities 1-by-1 to build and validate the complete 100% aggregate total).
* **Developer Analogy:** An `.every()` validation method—it checks every item individually, but does so to return a single verdict on the array as an aggregate whole.
* **Syntactic Behavior:** **Determiner Only ($D^0$)**. Strictly requires an overt head noun; cannot stand alone as a bare pronoun or take an immediate *of*-partitive phrase without nominal support.
  * *Determiner:* *"**Every** microservice was audited."*
  * *Crash:* ❌ *\*Every of the microservices was audited.*
  * *Repair (Compound / Analytic Head):* ✅ *"**Every one of the** microservices was audited."*

---

## 4. Deep Divergence Breakdown: Aggregate vs. Dual-Collective

### 1. Why *Every* is an Aggregate Distributive
* **The "Distributive" Requirement:** Like *each*, *every* enforces singular agreement (*Every node **is** healthy*), preventing the set from being treated as a collective plural entity.
* **The "Aggregate" Domain:** While *each* isolates Item A, then Item B, then Item C independently, *every* evaluates them 1-by-1 to exhaust the total collection:
$$\text{Item } A + \text{Item } B + \text{Item } C = 100\% \text{ of the entire domain } (N \ge 3)$$

### 2. Why *Both* is a Dual-Collective
* **The "Dual" Boundary:** *Both* is locked to a domain size of $N=2$.
* **The "Collective" Evaluation:** Unlike *either* and *neither*, which evaluate the two items disjunctively/distributively (enforcing singular agreement: *either route **is** fine*), **`both` evaluates the two items together as a combined joint set (+2)**.
* **Agreement Consequence:** Combining both entities into a joint set (+2) forces **plural verb agreement**:
  * *"**Both** servers **are** online."* (Not: ❌ *\*Both server is online*).

## 5. Scope of Negation: Dual vs. Multi-Domain Negative Logic

Negation shifts depending on whether the domain is dual ($N=2$) or multi-member ($N \ge 3$):

```text
Negation and Set Size Logic
├── Dual Domain (N = 2):
│   • Absolute Negation (0/2): "NEITHER replica was corrupted."
│   • Partial Negation ¬(A ∧ B): "We did not deploy BOTH features." (Deployed 1, not both).
│   • Disjunctive Negation:     "I cannot validate EITHER token."
│
└── Multi-Domain (N ≥ 3):
    • Absolute Negation (0/N): "NONE of the five pods failed."
    • Partial Negation ¬(All): "We did not deploy ALL features."
    • Arbitrary Universal:     "We don't trust ANY of the legacy modules."
```

## 6. Subject-Verb Agreement: Opaque Heads vs. Look-Through

Distributive quantifiers (*Each, Either, Neither*) are **opaque syntactic heads**. In partitive constructions, they block the "Look-Through" mechanism and enforce **Singular Concord**:

| Quantifier Construction        | Head Status              | Agreement Pathway                                            | Surface Example                                |
| :----------------------------- | :----------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| **`Both of [Plural DP]`**      | Dual-Collective Head     | Joint Set (+2) $\longrightarrow$ **Plural**                  | *"Both of the endpoints **are** responsive."*  |
| **`Either of [Plural DP]`**    | Opaque Distributive Head | Disjunctive ($1$ of $2$) $\longrightarrow$ **Singular**      | *"Either of the configurations **works**."*    |
| **`Neither of [Plural DP]`**   | Opaque Distributive Head | Universal Negative ($0$ of $2$) $\longrightarrow$ **Singular** | *"Neither of the scripts **handles** errors."* |
| **`Each of [Plural DP]`**      | Opaque Distributive Head | Isolated Singular Head $\longrightarrow$ **Singular**        | *"Each of the instances **requires** TLS."*    |
| **`Every one of [Plural DP]`** | Opaque Analytic Head     | Singular Head *one* $\longrightarrow$ **Singular**           | *"Every one of the pods **was** rebooted."*    |

## 7. Syntactic Crash Diagnostics

### Diagnostic 1: Dual-Domain Boundary Overflow ($N \ge 3$)
* ❌ *\*"**Neither of the four** worker nodes responded to the health probe."*
* **Root Cause:** *Neither* carries a strict selectional constraint restricted to $N=2$. Scaling to $N=4$ triggers an immediate domain collision.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"**None of the four** worker nodes responded to the health probe."*

### Diagnostic 2: Bare "Every" Partitive Crash
* ❌ *\*"**Every of the** deployment targets was successfully validated."*
* **Root Cause:** *Every* is exclusively a determiner ($D^0$) and cannot head an *of*-partitive phrase without an intervening nominal head.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"**Every one of the** deployment targets was successfully validated"* (or *"**Each of the** deployment targets..."*).

### Diagnostic 3: Opaque Head Agreement Leak
* ❌ *\*"**Neither of the two** API gateways **are** routing ingress traffic."*
* **Root Cause:** Proximity agreement leak. The speaker matches the verb to the plural noun *gateways* inside the prepositional phrase. *Neither* is an opaque head enforcing singular agreement.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"**Neither of the two** API gateways **is** routing ingress traffic."*

### Diagnostic 4: Number Feature Collision with "Both"
* ❌ *\*"**Both** network route **is** saturated."*
* **Root Cause:** *Both* is a dual-collective operator requiring a plural count head noun and a plural finite verb.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"**Both** network routes **are** saturated."*
