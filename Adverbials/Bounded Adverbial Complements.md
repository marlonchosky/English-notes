# Bounded & Restrictive Adverbial Complements

A **Bounded Adverbial Complement** is an obligatory prepositional phrase ($\text{PP}$) selected by a verb of containment, retention, or domain restriction that sets an **exclusive perimeter or non-spatial terminal boundary**.

Unlike directional complements marking motion toward an external target (e.g., *"send a letter to the team"*), restrictive bounded complements map an event's scope exclusively to a designated entity or private domain.

---

## 1. Syntax Guardrail: The Semantic Metaphor (Container & Scope)

In cognitive syntax, spatial prepositions (*to*, *within*, *in*) are extended from physical trajectories to abstract scope management:

* **Physical Locative Complement:** Directs an entity toward a geographic coordinate (*"He went to the office"*).
* **Bounded Restrictive Complement:** Treats the target argument as a **closed container / exclusive perimeter**. Deleting or redirecting the phrase violates the subcategorization frame of the governing verb.

$$\text{Event Scope} \subseteq \text{Domain Defined by } [\text{P}^\circ \text{ Complement}]$$

---

## 2. Core Structural Frames

### A. Complex Transitive Verb (CTV) + Reflexive Bounded Complement

Verbs of possession, retention, and containment (*keep*, *hold*, *restrict*) require both a Direct Object ($\text{DO}$) and an obligatory restrictive complement to enforce retention.

$$\text{[TP Subject [VP [V}^\circ\text{ keep] [}_{\text{DP}}\text{ DO] [}_{\text{PP}}\text{ Adverbial Complement of Bounded Domain]]}$$

* **Example:** *"Keep the secret **to yourself**."*
  * **keep:** Complex Transitive Verb ($\text{CTV} / V^\circ$)
  * **the secret:** Direct Object ($\text{DP}$)
  * **to yourself:** Prepositional Phrase ($\text{PP}$) functioning as an **Adverbial Complement of Bounded Domain**
    * **to:** Head Preposition ($P^\circ$) establishing boundary scope
    * **yourself:** Object of Preposition ($\text{DP}$, Reflexive Pronoun)

* **Diagnostic Test (Deletion Crash):**
  * *"Keep the secret."* $\rightarrow$ Shifts meaning from exclusive non-disclosure to simple preservation/maintenance. The restriction against outward transmission is lost.

---

### B. Complex Intransitive Verb (CIV) + Reflexive Bounded Complement

When verbs of movement, posture, or state shift to mark self-containment, they function as Complex Intransitive Verbs requiring an adverbial complement.

* **Example:** *"He **keeps** **to himself**."*
  * **Meaning:** He avoids social interaction and prefers solitude; he keeps his personal life, thoughts, and presence restricted to his own private sphere rather than engaging with others.
  * **keeps:** Complex Intransitive Verb ($\text{CIV} / V^\circ$)
  * **to himself:** Prepositional Phrase ($\text{PP}$) functioning as a **Locative Adverbial Complement of Social/Domain Boundary**

* **Diagnostic Test (Structural Crash):**
  * *\*"He keeps."* $\rightarrow$ **Syntactic Crash** ($\text{CIV}$ frame unfulfilled).

## 4. Complement vs. Adjunct Diagnostic

Apply the standard deletion and scope test:

1. **Sentence A (Complement):**
   * *"She kept the prize **to herself**."*
   * *Deletion:* *"She kept the prize."* (Grammatical, but radically alters argument semantics from *monopolization / refusal to share* to *retaining an item*).
2. **Sentence B (Adjunct):**
   * *"She read the book **by herself**."*
   * *Deletion:* *"She read the book."* (Core event is complete; *by herself* is an optional manner/agentive depictive adjunct).
