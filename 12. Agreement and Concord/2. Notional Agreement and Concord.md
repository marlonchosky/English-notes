# Notional Agreement and Concord: Semantic Agreement and The Look-Through Mechanism

**Notional Agreement** (also known in formal linguistics as *Notional Concord* or *Synesis*) is a grammatical phenomenon in which a finite verb agrees with the **semantic meaning (the underlying notion)** of the subject phrase rather than strictly conforming to the morphosyntactic number of the surface structural head.

While **Formal Concord** requires rigid alignment with the primary structural head noun, Notional Concord permits the verb to bypass surface packaging to reflect semantic quantities, collective internal agency, or partitioned substance.

---

## 1. Master Taxonomy: The Three Agreement Pathways

Every subject-verb configuration in English resolves through one of three agreement pathways:

| Agreement Pathway       | Operational Rule                                             | Agreement Mechanism                                          | Diagnostic Construction                                      |
| :---------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Formal Concord**      | Verb agrees with the surface grammatical head noun.          | **Structural Head Primacy** (blocks modifying phrases).      | *"The **list** of endpoints **is** corrupted."*              |
| **Notional Concord**    | Verb agrees with the perceived semantic entity or quantity.  | **Look-Through / Collective Shift** (targets underlying entity). | *"The **staff have** disagreed on the release date."*        |
| **Proximity Principle** | Verb agrees with the linearly closest noun (informal / speech). | **Linear Adjacency** (often triggers standard grammar crashes). | ⚠️ *"Either the architect or the **engineers are** on call."* |

## 2. The "Look-Through" Mechanism (Indefinite Quantifiers & Fractions)

When a subject is headed by an **indefinite quantifier** (*all, some, most, half, a fraction, none*), the quantifier itself lacks intrinsic, unalterable number features.

To resolve agreement, the finite verb activates the **Look-Through Mechanism**: it looks through the functional quantifier head and copies its agreement features directly from the nominal core inside the partitive *of*-phrase:

* **Quantifier + Mass Noun [-Count]** $\longrightarrow$ Verb is **SINGULAR**
  * *"All of the telemetry storage IS synchronized."*
  * *"Half of the codebase WAS refactored."*
  * *"Most of the memory HAS been freed."*
* **Quantifier + Plural Count Noun [+Count, +Plural]** $\longrightarrow$ Verb is **PLURAL**
  * *"All of the worker nodes ARE responsive."*
  * *"Half of the servers WERE rebooted."*
  * *"Most of the microservices HAVE passed health checks."*

### The Syntactic Structural Contrast: Configuration A vs. Configuration B

The look-through effect occurs only when an overt partitive prepositional phrase is projected:

1. **Configuration A (With "Of" - Partitive Pronoun Construction):**  
   `[DP [Head All] [PP [P° of] [DP the data]]]`  
   *All* functions as an indefinite pronoun head; *of the data* is an adnominal partitive phrase. Agreement resolves **notionally** via the Look-Through Mechanism (looks through to *data* $\longrightarrow$ *is*).

2. **Configuration B (Without "Of" - Pre-Determiner / Direct Determiner):**  
   `[DP [Spec All] [D' [D° the] [NP data]]]`  
   *All* is a functional pre-determiner and *data* is the direct structural head noun of the Determiner Phrase. Agreement resolves **formally** via Head Primacy (*data* $\longrightarrow$ *is*).

## 3. Collective Nouns: Institutional Unit vs. Individual Members

Collective nouns (*team, committee, staff, crew, family, government, board*) denote a single organizational group comprised of multiple individual agents. English syntax licenses an agreement split based on **speaker perspective**:

* **Formal Concord (Unified Institutional Unit):**
  * Singular verb agreement.
  * Focus: Single corporate entity.
  * Co-indexed Pronoun: *it / its*.
  * *"The team HAS released ITS report."*
* **Notional Concord (Independent Individual Actors):**
  * Plural verb agreement.
  * Focus: Distinct people in conflict, negotiation, or individual action.
  * Co-indexed Pronoun: *they / their*.
  * *"The team HAVE disagreed among THEMSELVES."*

### Dialectal Divergence (American English vs. British English)
* **American English (US):** Heavily defaults to **Formal Concord (Singular)** for collective nouns (*"The committee is deciding"*), reserving plural agreement for instances of overt internal conflict (*"The committee are split"*).
* **British English (UK):** Readily embraces **Notional Concord (Plural)** whenever human action, volition, or multi-member participation is implied (*"The team are deploying the update"*, *"The government are debating"*).

## 4. Opaque Heads vs. Transparent Quantifiers

A critical syntactic crash occurs when speakers apply the look-through mechanism to an **Opaque Lexical Head**. Unlike transparent functional quantifiers (*all of, half of*), lexical measurement, collective, and boundary nouns enforce **Strict Head Primacy**:

| Subject Construction          | Head Status                  | Governing Mechanism           | Verb Agreement | Example Construction                       |
| :---------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------: | :----------------------------------------- |
| **`All of [the code]`**       | Transparent Quantifier       | Look-Through (Mass)           |  **Singular**  | *"All of the code **is** refactored."*     |
| **`All of [the pods]`**       | Transparent Quantifier       | Look-Through (Plural)         |   **Plural**   | *"All of the pods **are** ready."*         |
| **`The list of [pods]`**      | **Opaque Lexical Head**      | Formal Concord (Head Primacy) |  **Singular**  | *"The list of pods **is** verified."*      |
| **`Two liters of [coolant]`** | **Opaque Measure Head**      | Formal Concord (Head Primacy) |   **Plural**   | *"Two liters of coolant **are** inside."*  |
| **`Each of [the clusters]`**  | **Opaque Distributive Head** | Formal Concord ($N \ge 2$)    |  **Singular**  | *"Each of the clusters **requires** TLS."* |
| **`Neither of [the nodes]`**  | **Opaque Dual Head**         | Formal Concord ($N = 2$)      |  **Singular**  | *"Neither of the nodes **fails**."*        |

## 5. Scalar Measures of Time, Distance, and Money

When a plural measurement phrase functions as a semantic predicate of duration, monetary cost, weight, or distance, it behaves notionally as a **single unified scalar block**, forcing **singular verb agreement**:

$$\mathbf{\text{[Plural Measure Phrase]}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Evaluated as a Single Continuous Magnitude}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Singular Verb}}$$

* *"**Ten minutes** **is** sufficient to execute the rollback script."*  
  $\rightarrow$ Not 10 individual minutes acting independently; it represents a single unified block of elapsed time.
* *"**Five thousand dollars** **was** spent on infrastructure hosting."*  
  $\rightarrow$ Evaluated as a single monetary sum, not 5,000 individual dollar bills.
* *"**Fifty miles** **is** too far to run fiber without signal repeaters."*  
  $\rightarrow$ Evaluated as a single continuous distance vector.

## 6. Syntactic Crash Diagnostics

### Diagnostic 1: False Look-Through on Opaque Noun Heads
* ❌ *\*"The **inventory** of server instances **have** become inconsistent."*
* **Root Cause:** Proximity agreement leak. The speaker looks through to the plural nominal *instances*. *Inventory* is an opaque lexical noun head; it blocks look-through and mandates singular agreement.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The **inventory** of server instances **has** become inconsistent."*

### Diagnostic 2: Number Feature Collision in Collective Pronouns
* ❌ *\*"The **infrastructure team has** revoked the keys because **they** were compromised."* (Valid) vs. ❌ *\*"The **team have** published **its** decision."*
* **Root Cause:** Pronoun-verb discord. Mixing a plural notional verb (*have*) with a singular neuter pronoun (*its*) triggers an agreement feature clash within the same discourse domain.
* ✅ **Fix (Consistent Formal):** *"The **team has** published **its** decision."*
* ✅ **Fix (Consistent Notional):** *"The **team have** published **their** decision."*

### Diagnostic 3: Mass Look-Through Violation
* ❌ *\*"Most of the database **indexing were** completed during off-peak hours."*
* **Root Cause:** *Most of* is transparent and inspects *indexing*. Because *indexing* is an uncountable mass nominal ($[-\text{Count}]$), copying plural agreement causes a morphosyntactic crash.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"Most of the database **indexing was** completed during off-peak hours."*

### Diagnostic 4: Opaque Distributive Operator Leak
* ❌ *\*"**Every one** of the target microservices **were** restarted."*
* **Root Cause:** The analytic distributive head *one* is strictly $[+\text{Singular}]$. Distributive operators evaluate members 1-by-1 in isolation and block plural look-through.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"**Every one** of the target microservices **was** restarted."*
