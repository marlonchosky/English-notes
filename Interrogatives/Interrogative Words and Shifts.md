# Interrogatives: Morphosyntactic Roles & Distribution

Interrogative words (commonly called *wh-words*) are functional operators used to form direct and indirect questions. Depending on their syntactic position, they shift between three core word classes: **Determiners**, **Pronouns**, and **Adverbs**.

---

## 1. The Functional Split Matrix

| Word           | Can act as Determiner? | Can act as Pronoun? | Can act as Adverb? | Primary Function / Scope               |
| :------------- | :--------------------: | :-----------------: | :----------------: | :------------------------------------- |
| **What**       |          YES           |         YES         |         NO         | Open-ended choice / Non-bounded domain |
| **Which**      |          YES           |         YES         |         NO         | Closed choice / Bounded domain         |
| **Whose**      |          YES           |         YES         |         NO         | Genitive / Possessive dependency       |
| **Who / Whom** |           NO           |         YES         |         NO         | Animate entity (Subject / Object)      |
| **Where**      |           NO           |         NO          |        YES         | Locative coordinate (Spatial)          |
| **When**       |           NO           |         NO          |        YES         | Temporal coordinate (Time)             |
| **Why**        |           NO           |         NO          |        YES         | Causal / Reason                        |
| **How**        |           NO           |         NO          |        YES         | Manner / Degree operator               |

---

## 2. Interrogative Determiners vs. Interrogative Pronouns

Interrogative words that target nominals (`what`, `which`, `whose`) exhibit a **Determiner-to-Pronoun shift** based on whether an overt head noun is present in the Determiner Phrase ($DP$).

### A. Interrogative Determiners ($D^0$)
Operate as specifiers at the left edge of a $DP$, requiring an overt head noun.
* **What:** *"[To] **What** extent are you willing to test?"* (Open choice modifying *extent*)
* **Which:** *"**Which** database failed?"* (Bounded selection modifying *database*)
* **Whose:** *"**Whose** commit broke the build?"* (Genitive specifier modifying *commit*)

### B. Interrogative Pronouns ($NP$ Head)
Operate as the sole structural Head of an $NP$ when the target noun is omitted or inherently represented.
* **What:** *"**What** did you say?"* (Replaces the entire $DP$ object)
* **Which:** *"**Which** of these is faster?"* (Functions as a partitive pronoun head)
* **Whose:** *"**Whose** is this?"* (Stands alone as a possessive pronoun head)

---

## 3. Structural Diagnostics & Guardrails

### 1. Modern Linguistics vs. Traditional Grammar
* **Traditional Label:** Interrogative Adjective (when modifying a noun).
* **Modern Syntactic Label:** Interrogative Determiner.
* **Why Modern Syntax Wins:** Unlike true adjectives, interrogative determiners cannot be modified by degree adverbs (`*very what extent`) and cannot stack with central determiners (`*the what extent`).

### 2. Bounded vs. Unbounded Domain Selection
* **Which (Bounded):** Used when selecting from a known, closed set.
  * *Example:* *"Which endpoint returned 500?"* (Implicitly out of a set of known endpoints)
* **What (Unbounded):** Used for open-ended, unspecified queries.
  * *Example:* *"What language is the service written in?"* (Open domain)

---

## 4. Prepositional Phrases & Adverbials (`To what extent`)

In prepositional structures like **"To what extent"**:
* **To:** Preposition (Head of $PP$).
* **what:** Interrogative Determiner modifying *extent*.
* **extent:** Head Noun (Object of the preposition).
* **Syntactic Result:** The entire $PP$ operates as an **Adverbial of Degree or Measure**.
