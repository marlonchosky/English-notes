# Evaluative Adjectives (Adjectives of Value, Opinion, and Mental Judgment)

An **Evaluative Adjective** expresses a speaker’s subjective opinion, mental representation, psychological attitude, or qualitative judgment of a nominal entity (*intelligent, flawed, intuitive, terrible, acceptable*), rather than an objective, measurable physical property (*wooden, circular, five-meter*).

Unlike a **Resultative** (which denotes a physical, objective change of state), an evaluative adjective applies a **mental label or subjective assessment**.

---

## 1. Syntactic Structures & Predication Frames

Evaluative adjectives operate across three primary clausal architectures, categorized by their syntactic function (**Complement vs. Adjunct**) and predication type:

```text
Evaluative Adjective Clausal Distribution
├── A. Copular / Linking Frames:      Subject Complement (Primary Predicate)
├── B. Complex Transitive Frames:     Evaluative Depictive Complement (Object Complement)
└── C. Secondary Depictive Frames:    Evaluative Depictive Adjunct (Optional Secondary Predicate)
```

### A. Copular / Linking Structures (Subject Complements)
The evaluative adjective functions as the primary **Subject Complement ($\text{SC}$)**, attributing a subjective property directly to the grammatical subject via a linking verb (*be, seem, remain, prove*):
* *The proposed database schema is **flawed**.*
* *The telemetry dashboard remains **intuitive** after the redesign.*
* *The containerized caching layer proved **robust** during stress testing.*

### B. Complex Transitive Structures (Evaluative Depictive Complements)
Verbs of mental judgment, perception, and subjective evaluation (*consider, deem, find, judge*) license a Complex Transitive Verb ($\text{CTV}$) frame:

$$\text{[TP Subject [VP [V}^\circ\ \text{CTV]} \ \text{[}_{\text{DP}}\ \text{DO]} \ \text{[}_{\text{AP}}\ \text{Evaluative Depictive Complement}]]] \quad (\text{Subject} + \text{CTV} + \text{DO} + \text{OC})$$

* *"The systems architect considered the deployment strategy **unviable**."*
  * **considered:** Complex Transitive Verb ($\text{CTV}$) of mental evaluation.
  * **the deployment strategy:** Direct Object ($\text{DO}$).
  * **unviable:** **Evaluative Depictive Complement** (Object Complement).
* *"The review committee found the vendor proposal **acceptable**."*
* *"The security auditor deemed the encryption configuration **inadequate**."*

> 💡 **Semantic Status:** Even though this constituent occupies the obligatory **Object Complement** slot, its semantic role is **Depictive** (not Resultative). It characterizes the perceived state of the direct object *concurrently with the act of evaluation*, rather than marking a caused physical transformation.

### C. Depictive Secondary Predication (Evaluative Depictive Adjuncts)
The evaluative adjective attaches to a complete intransitive or transitive predicate as an optional **Depictive Adjunct**, describing the emotional state, psychological mindset, or evaluative disposition of a participant *during* the action:
* *The platform engineer left the retrospective **pessimistic**.* (Subject-Oriented Evaluative Adjunct).
* *The lead developer delivered the keynote **confident**.* (Subject-Oriented Evaluative Adjunct).
* *They married **young**.* (Subject-Oriented Evaluative Adjunct).

## 2. The Stative vs. Dynamic Constraint (The CTV Constraint)

Evaluative adjectives are predominantly **stative**—they characterize an enduring essence, stable property, or mental representation of an entity:

* **Stative Quality:** *The system is **resilient**.* (Stable property / judgment).
* **Dynamic Behavior:** *He is **being irresponsible**.* (Temporary, volitional acting out).

### The CTV Mental Evaluation Rule
Complex Transitive Verbs of judgment (*consider, deem, find, judge*) **strictly require stative evaluative readings**. Pairing them with progressive dynamic markers (*being + adjective*) denotes temporary volitional behavior and causes an immediate structural crash:

* ✅ *"The security committee deemed the engineer **irresponsible** during the outage."* (Stative property).
* ❌ *\*"The security committee deemed the engineer **being irresponsible** during the outage."* (Dynamic progressive crash).
* ✅ *"The audit panel judged the deployment pipeline **vulnerable**."*
* ❌ *\*"The audit panel judged the deployment pipeline **being vulnerable**."* (Crash).

## 3. Passive Voice Transformations & Role Reassignment

When a Complex Transitive sentence containing an Evaluative Object Complement is passivized, the structural argument positions shift systematically:

1. The underlying **Direct Object ($\text{DO}$)** promotes to matrix **Subject ($\text{Spec, TP}$)**.
2. The **Evaluative Object Complement ($\text{OC}$)** is reassigned to the role of **Evaluative Subject Complement ($\text{SC}$)**.

```text
Active CTV Frame:
[The lead architect] considered [the microservice prototype] [unviable].
      (Subject)           (CTV)               (Direct Object)         (Object Complement)

Passive Copular/Participial Frame:
[The microservice prototype] was considered [unviable] (by the architect).
         (Subject)            (Passive Verb)   (Subject Complement)
```

| Active Construction (CTV + Object Complement)           | Passive Construction                          | New Function of Adjective         |
| :------------------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| *The QA team found the test suite **ineffective**.*     | *The test suite was found **ineffective**.*   | **Evaluative Subject Complement** |
| *The board deemed the migration **critical**.*          | *The migration was deemed **critical**.*      | **Evaluative Subject Complement** |
| *The committee judged the codebase **unmaintainable**.* | *The codebase was judged **unmaintainable**.* | **Evaluative Subject Complement** |

## 4. The Unaccusative Crash Diagnostic (Evaluative Prohibition)

In generative grammar, **Unaccusative Verbs** (*break, freeze, melt, crash, shatter*) denote an involuntary, physical change of state where the grammatical subject is an underlying patient/theme.

### The Evaluative Prohibition Rule
Because unaccusative changes of state demand an **objective, physical resultative endpoint** (such as *flat, solid, open,* or a PP like *into pieces*), they **strictly reject evaluative adjectives as secondary predicates**:

* ❌ *\*"The server cluster crashed **terrible** during peak traffic."*  
  $\rightarrow$ **Why it crashes:** A cluster cannot be depictively *terrible* during a crash, and an evaluative adjective cannot serve as a physical resultative endpoint.  
  $\rightarrow$ **Valid Alternative:** *The server cluster crashed **terribly**.* (Manner Adverbial modifying the verb event).
* ❌ *\*"The delicate porcelain insulator shattered **magnificent** on impact."*  
  $\rightarrow$ **Why it crashes:** *Shatter* requires physical fragmentation; subjective mental evaluations (*magnificent*) collide with the unaccusative result frame.  
  $\rightarrow$ **Valid Alternative:** *The porcelain insulator shattered **magnificently**.* (Adverb) OR *shattered **into pieces**.* (Prepositional Resultative Complement).
* ✅ *"The secondary coolant line froze **solid** under sub-zero temperatures."*  
  $\rightarrow$ **Valid:** *Solid* is an objective physical resultative adjective properly bounding the unaccusative verb *froze*.

## 5. Analytical Guardrails & Boundary Diagnostics

### Guardrail 1: Evaluative Depictive vs. Physical Depictive
Distinguish subjective mental attitudes from literal, objective physical conditions concurrent with the action:
* *The engineer attended the incident bridge **furious**.* $\rightarrow$ **Evaluative/Mental Depictive** (Psychological mindset / emotional state).
* *The technician inspected the blade server **undamaged**.* $\rightarrow$ **Physical Depictive** (Objective material condition).
* *The administrator drank the dark espresso **cold**.* $\rightarrow$ **Physical Depictive** (Objective thermal state).
* *The systems architect proposed the migration **optimistic**.* $\rightarrow$ **Evaluative/Mental Depictive** (Attitudinal disposition).

### Guardrail 2: Depictive Complement vs. Depictive Adjunct (The Deletion Diagnostic)
* **Evaluative Complement:** Deleting the adjective crashes the clause or alters the lexical meaning of the verb from mental evaluation to physical discovery:
  * *"The QA team found the legacy test harness **unusable**."*  
    $\rightarrow$ *Deletion:* *"The QA team found the legacy test harness."* (Alters meaning from *judged to be* to physical discovery/location $\rightarrow$ **Complement**).
* **Evaluative Adjunct:** Deleting the adjective leaves the primary predication semantically and grammatically complete:
  * *"The engineer delivered the technical presentation **confident**."*  
    $\rightarrow$ *Deletion:* *"The engineer delivered the technical presentation."* (Clause remains complete $\rightarrow$ **Adjunct**).
