# Adjective Ordering, Heavy APs, and Positional Constraints

Adjectives and Adjective Phrases ($\text{AP}$) modify nouns or pronouns, but their positioning within a Determiner Phrase ($\text{DP}$) or clause structure is strictly regulated by syntactic constraints and phrasal weight rules.

---

## 1. Canonical Adjective Ordering: OSASCOMP

When multiple pre-nominal adjectives modify a single head noun within a Determiner Phrase ($\text{DP}$), English enforces a hierarchical ordering system known as **OSASCOMP**. Deviating from this order produces unnatural or illicit sequences.

$$\text{Opinion} \longrightarrow \text{Size} \longrightarrow \text{Age} \longrightarrow \text{Shape} \longrightarrow \text{Color} \longrightarrow \text{Origin} \longrightarrow \text{Material} \longrightarrow \text{Purpose}$$

| Order | Semantic Category   | Operational Diagnostic                    | Examples                                        | Sample Mapping |
| :---: | :------------------ | :---------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- | :------------- |
| **1** | **Opinion / Value** | Subjective assessment / mental evaluation | *useful, sleek, robust, horrible, expensive*    | *sleek*        |
| **2** | **Size**            | Physical dimensions / magnitude           | *large, small, compact, massive, short*         | *small*        |
| **3** | **Age**             | Chronological maturity / era              | *new, modern, legacy, antique, ancient*         | *modern*       |
| **4** | **Shape**           | Geometric contour / form                  | *cylindrical, rectangular, square, round*       | *rectangular*  |
| **5** | **Color**           | Visual hue / chromatic property           | *blue, dark, green, white*                      | *dark*         |
| **6** | **Origin**          | Geographic source / platform              | *Japanese, German, Swiss, cloud-hosted*         | *Japanese*     |
| **7** | **Material**        | Physical substance / composition          | *metal, plastic, copper, gold, wooden*          | *metal*        |
| **8** | **Purpose / Type**  | Function / Noun adjunct classifier        | *server, caching, grounding, security, testing* | *server*       |

### Example Construction
> *"We installed a **sleek, small, modern, rectangular, dark, Japanese, metal, server** rack."*

### Noun Adjuncts vs. Classifying Adjectives
Words occupying the **Purpose / Type (Position 8)** slot are frequently **Noun Adjuncts** (nouns acting morphosyntactically as pre-modifiers, such as *server rack*, *database schema*, *security policy*). They must sit immediately adjacent to the head noun, following all true adjectives.

## 2. Heavy Adjective Phrases ($\text{AP}$) and Phrasal Weight Shifts

While single-word lexical adjectives ($A^\circ$) sit pre-nominally, an Adjective Phrase that contains its own internal **complement** (a Prepositional Phrase or Infinitive Clause) becomes **syntactically heavy** and triggers the **Heavy AP Post-Nominal Shift**.

```text
The Phrasal Weight Rule
├── Light AP (Single Head / Degree + Head):  [DP a [AP resilient] node]        ──► Valid Pre-Nominal
└── Heavy AP (Head + PP / Clausal Comp):     *[DP a [AP resilient to load] node] ──► CRASH (Must Shift)
```

### A. Prepositional Phrase Complements
* ❌ *\*We deployed a [capable of high throughput] system.* (Pre-nominal heavy AP crash)
* ✅ *We deployed a system **[capable of high throughput]**.*
* ❌ *\*The team decommissioned a [vulnerable to buffer overflow] server.*
* ✅ *The team decommissioned a server **[vulnerable to buffer overflow]**.*

### B. Infinitival Clause Complements
* ❌ *\*He is an [eager to learn C#] developer.* (Pre-nominal heavy AP crash)
* ✅ *He is a developer **[eager to learn C#]**.*
* ❌ *\*We resolved a [hard to debug] race condition.*
* ✅ *We resolved a race condition **[hard to debug]**.*

### C. Syntactic Alternation: The Hyphenated Pre-Nominal Compound
To retain a heavy concept in the pre-nominal position, English allows morphosyntactic re-packaging into an indivisible **hyphenated compound modifier**:
* *A system [capable of scaling]* $\longrightarrow$ *A **scale-capable** system.*
* *A server [vulnerable to overflow]* $\longrightarrow$ *An **overflow-vulnerable** server.*
* *An issue [hard to debug]* $\longrightarrow$ *A **hard-to-debug** issue.*

## 3. Postpositive Adjectives with Compound Indefinite Pronouns

Compound indefinite pronouns (*someone, somebody, something, anyone, anybody, anything, no one, nobody, nothing*) represent fused, indivisible nominal heads ($\text{DP/NP}$). 

Because their morphological head is inherently fused with a determiner component, they **reject pre-determiners and pre-nominal attributive adjectives**, forcing all modifying adjectives into the **postpositive position**:

$$\mathbf{\text{Compound Indefinite Pronoun}} + \mathbf{\text{Adjective Phrase (AP)}}$$

* ❌ *\*The monitoring system flagged an alarming something.*
* ✅ *The monitoring system flagged **something alarming**.*
* ❌ *\*Did the security scan identify critical anything?*
* ✅ *Did the security scan identify **anything critical**?*
* ❌ *\*We encountered an unexpected nothing during the table audit.*
* ✅ *We encountered **nothing unexpected** during the table audit.*

## 4. Distributional Restrictions: Attributive-Only vs. Predicate-Only

Not all adjectives can freely alternate between attributive and predicative slots. Modern syntax recognizes two strictly restricted subsets:

### A. Predicate-Only Adjectives ($a$-adjectives)
Adjectives prefixed with historical locative/state $a$- (*asleep, awake, afraid, alive, alone, ashamed*) cannot function as pre-nominal attributive modifiers. They are restricted to predicate positions following copular or linking verbs:

| Illicit Attributive Slot (Crash)                   | Valid Copular Predicate Slot       | Valid Attributive Replacement                       |
| :------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| ❌ *\*An **asleep** operator missed the alert.*     | ✅ *The operator was **asleep**.*   | ✅ *A **sleeping** operator missed the alert.*       |
| ❌ *\*An **afraid** engineer postponed the deploy.* | ✅ *The engineer was **afraid**.*   | ✅ *A **frightened** engineer postponed the deploy.* |
| ❌ *\*The **alone** developer resolved the outage.* | ✅ *The developer was **alone**.*   | ✅ *The **solitary / lone** developer resolved it.*  |
| ❌ *\*An **alive** process holds the file lock.*    | ✅ *The process remains **alive**.* | ✅ *An **active / live** process holds the lock.*    |

### B. Attributive-Only Adjectives
Non-gradable, intensifying, or relational adjectives (*mere, utter, chief, sheer, main, principal*) cannot stand alone in the predicate complement position behind a linking verb:

| Illicit Predicate Slot (Crash)        | Valid Attributive Position                   | Valid Predicative Replacement                |
| :------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| ❌ *\*The error was **mere**.*         | ✅ *It was a **mere** syntax error.*          | ✅ *The error was **minor / negligible**.*    |
| ❌ *\*The failure was **utter**.*      | ✅ *The deployment was an **utter** failure.* | ✅ *The failure was **complete / absolute**.* |
| ❌ *\*The reason was **chief**.*       | ✅ *The **chief** reason was disk failure.*   | ✅ *The reason was **primary / central**.*    |
| ❌ *\*The worker process is **main**.* | ✅ *The **main** process handled requests.*   | ✅ *The worker process is **primary**.*       |

## 5. Analytical Guardrails & Boundary Diagnostics

### Guardrail 1: Multiple Opinion Adjectives
When stacking multiple adjectives of opinion or evaluation, sub-ordering proceeds from **General/Subjective Opinion** to **Specific/Observable Quality**:
* *A **useful, efficient** database driver.* (*useful* = subjective utility $\rightarrow$ *efficient* = quantifiable operational feature).

### Guardrail 2: Commas vs. No Commas (Coordinate vs. Cumulative Adjectives)
* **Coordinate Adjectives (Separated by Commas):** Adjectives from the *same* OSASCOMP category that independently modify the noun (can be reordered or linked with *and*):
  * *"A **resilient, stable** architecture."* $\longrightarrow$ *"A **stable and resilient** architecture."*
* **Cumulative Adjectives (No Commas):** Adjectives from *different* OSASCOMP tiers that build upon each other hierarchically (cannot be reordered or linked with *and*):
  * *"A **modern Linux** server."* (Age $\rightarrow$ Type).
  * ❌ *\*A modern and Linux server.*

### Guardrail 3: Compound Indefinite Pronoun vs. Independent Post-Modifier
Do not confuse an adjective post-modifying a compound indefinite pronoun with a secondary predicate modifying a VP:
* *We found [DP **something strange**].* $\longrightarrow$ Postpositive attributive modifier inside the direct object DP.
* *We found the system **strange**.* $\longrightarrow$ Evaluative Depictive Object Complement inside a Complex Transitive Verb frame.
