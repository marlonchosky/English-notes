# Adverbials of Time and Frequency

An **Adverbial of Time** situates an event, state, or proposition along the chronological continuum. It addresses two fundamental inquiries:
* **When?** (Temporal Anchor / Point in Time)
* **How often?** (Frequency / Recurrence Rate)

---

## 1. Syntactic Function: Adjunct vs. Complement Status

In the vast majority of clause architectures, temporal expressions function as optional **Adverbial Adjuncts**. However, verbs of persistence, duration, and scheduling subcategorize for obligatory **Temporal Complements**.

```text
Temporal Functional Distribution
├── Adverbial Adjunct (Optional):    "The backup starts [PP at midnight]."
└── Adverbial Complement (Required): "The maintenance window lasted [PP until midnight]."
```

### A. Temporal Adjuncts (Passes Deletion Diagnostic)
The base predicate is syntactically complete; omitting the temporal constituent leaves a fully grammatical clause:
* *"The backup starts **[PP at midnight]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *Deletion:* *"The backup starts."* ✅ (Grammatically intact intransitive frame; *at midnight* provides optional temporal context).
* *"We deploy code **[AdvP bi-weekly]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *Deletion:* *"We deploy code."* ✅ (Monotransitive frame remains saturated).

### B. Temporal Complements (Fails Deletion Diagnostic)
Complex Intransitive Verbs (CIVs) expressing duration (*last*, *persist*, *remain*) demand a temporal bound or measure:
* *"The shift lasted **[PP until midnight]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *Deletion:* *\*"The shift lasted."* ❌ (Syntactic crash; *last* obligatorily selects a temporal/duration complement).

## 2. Temporal Sub-Classifications

### 1. Specific Coordinate Points (Point in Time / Anchor)
Pinpoints an exact coordinate on the clock face, calendar, or relative to the speech event ($Point\ A$):
* *He arrived **[AdvP yesterday]**.*
* *The cron job triggers **[PP at 02:00 UTC]**.*
* *The team deployed the hotfix **[DP this morning]**.*

### 2. Frequency and Iteration
Measures recurrence intervals and distribution over time:
* **Indefinite Frequency:** Expressed via degree/frequency adverbs (*always*, *frequently*, *rarely*, *often*).
  * *The worker thread **[AdvP frequently]** stalls.*
* **Definite / Periodic Recurrence:** Expressed via complex nominals or prepositional loops:
  * *We run health checks **[DP every ten seconds]**.*
  * *The build executes **[AdvP bi-weekly]**.*

### 3. Temporal Boundaries (Terminal / Horizon Limits)
Prepositional heads determine whether the temporal frame is forward-looking or retrospective:
* **Terminal Endpoint (`to`, `until`, `till`):** Sets a terminal horizon.
  * *The engineer monitored logs **[PP until sunrise]**.*
* **Origin Boundary (`since`, `from`):** Sets the inception point looking forward.
  * *Telemetry has reported spikes **[PP since morning]**.*

---

## 3. Structural Realizations ("Data Types")

The temporal adverbial slot can be filled across four distinct syntactic categories:

| Structural Form                        | Lexical / Syntactic Anatomy                | Example Construction                         | Clausal Syntactic Role     |
| :------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------- |
| **Adverb Phrase ($\text{AdvP}$)**      | Head temporal/frequency adverb ($Adv^0$)   | *He arrived **yesterday**.*                  | Temporal Adjunct           |
| **Prepositional Phrase ($\text{PP}$)** | Head $P^0$ + Nominal complement            | *He arrived **at noon**.*                    | Temporal Adjunct           |
| **Determiner Phrase ($\text{DP}$)**    | Bare nominal expressing time without $P^0$ | *He arrived **this morning**.*               | Temporal Adjunct (Bare DP) |
| **Subordinate Clause ($\text{CP}$)**   | Subordinating conjunction + Finite clause  | *He arrived **before the meeting started**.* | Temporal Clausal Adjunct   |

## 4. X-Bar Anatomy: Bare Temporal DPs & Interrogative Operators

### A. Bare Temporal Nominals ($\text{DP}$ Functioning Adverbially)
Unlike direct objects that occupy core argument positions, bare temporal DPs adjoin to the VP/TP level:

```text
       VP
      /  \
    VP    DP (Temporal Adjunct)
   /        \
[V° ran]   [DP this morning]
```
Even though *this morning* has the internal category of a Determiner Phrase, its external functional role is an **Adverbial Adjunct of Time**.

### B. Interrogative Frequency Phrases (`How often`)
Under standard X-Bar projection, interrogative operators query frequency by sitting in the Specifier position of the $\text{AdvP}$:

```text
       AdvP (Frequency Adverbial Container)
      /    \
Spec, AdvP   Adv'
 (Operator)  |
   How      Adv° (Head)
           often
```
* *"**[AdvP How often]** does the pipeline run?"*
  * **how:** Interrogative Degree Adverb sitting in $\text{Spec, AdvP}$.
  * **often:** Head Frequency Adverb ($Adv^0$).

## 5. Analytical Guardrails & Boundary Tests

### Guardrail 1: Temporal Coordinate (Time) vs. Temporal Span (Measure)
* **Temporal Coordinate ($Point\ A$ on Timeline):** Answers *When?*
  * *"The deployment finishes **at 5:00 PM**."* $\rightarrow$ **Adverbial of Time**.
* **Temporal Span ($\Delta t$ on Scalar Axis):** Answers *How long?*
  * *"The deployment lasted **for five hours**."* $\rightarrow$ **Adverbial of Measure / Duration**.

### Guardrail 2: Clock Points vs. Ordinal Sequence (The Determiner Crash)
Absolute clock coordinates and solar landmarks behave like proper nouns and reject the central determiner (*the*):
* ✅ *The script ran **until midnight**.*
* ❌ *\*The script ran **until the midnight**.* (Structural Crash: Clock points reject central $D^0$).

### Guardrail 3: Direct Object DP vs. Adverbial Temporal DP
Verbs like *spend* or *waste* take temporal DPs as **Direct Objects** (transitive arguments), whereas verbs like *walk* or *sleep* take temporal DPs as **Adverbial Adjuncts**:
* *He wasted **three hours**.* $\rightarrow$ *"Three hours were wasted."* ✅ (Passivization succeeds $\rightarrow$ Direct Object DP).
* *He slept **three hours**.* $\rightarrow$ *\*"Three hours were slept."* ❌ (Passivization fails $\rightarrow$ Temporal Measure Adverbial).
