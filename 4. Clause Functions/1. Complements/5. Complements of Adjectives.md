# Complements of Adjectives (Adjectival Complements)

An **Adjective Complement** (or **Adjectival Complement**) is an obligatory or structurally licensed argument that attaches directly inside an **Adjective Phrase ($\text{AP}$)** to complete the lexical semantics of the head adjective ($A^\circ$).

It answers:
* **"In what respect?" / "In what domain?"** (*good [at math]*, *quick [to respond]*)
* **"Of what?" / "Target stimulus?"** (*afraid [of failure]*, *aware [of the bug]*)
* **"Why?" / "Trigger fact?"** (*glad [that the tests passed]*)

Unlike an optional clausal or adverbial adjunct, removing an adjective complement results in an incomplete proposition or alters the lexical sense of the adjective.

---

## 1. Structural Categories of Adjective Complements

Adjective Complements distribute across five primary structural configurations:

| Structural Realization                           | Selecting Adjective Class                  | Syntactic Role & Mechanism                            | Canonical Example Construction                        |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prepositional Phrase ($\text{PP}$)**           | Emotional trigger, capacity, attitude      | Prepositional hook selected by lexical $A^\circ$      | *She is very good **at math**.*                       |
| **Infinitival Clause (Standard Control)**        | Intent, volition, emotional readiness      | Non-finite clause with subject control ($\text{PRO}$) | *We are ready **to deploy the update**.*              |
| **Infinitival Clause (Tough-Movement)**          | Ease, difficulty, evaluative quality       | Non-finite clause with an object gap ($\emptyset$)    | *This bug is difficult **to reproduce $\emptyset$**.* |
| **Bare Present Participle Phrase**               | State of occupation or immersion           | Direct non-finite complement without $P^\circ$        | *The worker is busy **indexing tables**.*             |
| **Finite Content Clause ($\text{That}$-Clause)** | Cognitive certainty, mental state, emotion | Finite propositional explication of state             | *I am certain **that the server rebooted**.*          |

---

## 2. Deep Dive by Structural Realization

### A. Prepositional Phrase Complements ($\text{PP}$)
Many adjectives require an idiosyncratic prepositional "hook" to license an overt nominal or gerund argument:
* *The team is afraid **[PP of making schema alterations]**.*
* *The lead architect is intent **[PP on automating the rollout]**.*
* *The candidate is qualified **[PP for the role]**.*

### B. Infinitival Clause Complements: Standard Control vs. Tough-Movement
Infinitival complements split into two fundamentally distinct syntactic operations based on argument gap licensing:

```text
Infinitival Adjective Complements
├── 1. Standard Subject Control (PRO): [TP The team_i is ready [CP PRO_i to deploy]]
└── 2. Tough-Movement (Object Gap):    [TP The code_i is difficult [CP PRO to parse t_i]]
```

#### 1. Standard Subject Control ($\text{PRO}$)
The matrix subject is the semantic **Agent** performing the action of the infinitival verb. The infinitive contains an unexpressed subject pronoun ($\text{PRO}$) co-indexed with the matrix subject:
* *The developers are eager **[CP $\text{PRO}_i$ to refactor the monolithic service]**.*
* *She is hesitant **[CP $\text{PRO}_i$ to grant production access]**.*

#### 2. Tough-Movement (Tough-Complements)
Selected by adjectives of ease, difficulty, or experiential evaluation (*tough, easy, hard, difficult, impossible, fun*). 
* The matrix surface subject is **not** the agent of the infinitive; it is the **underlying Direct Object or Prepositional Object** of the lower verb that has been fronted to subject position.
* The infinitival clause contains a mandatory **empty structural gap / trace ($\emptyset$)**:
  * Deep Base: *It is difficult [to reproduce **this race condition**].*
  * Surface Tough-Movement: ***This race condition**$_i$ is difficult [CP to reproduce $t_i$].*
  * Stranded Preposition Gap: ***This architecture**$_i$ is fun [CP to work with $t_i$].*

### C. Bare Present Participle Phrases ($\text{-ing}$ Complements)
A select subset of adjectives (*busy, worth, trapped*) possesses the lexical property of directly selecting a bare present participle phrase without an intervening preposition:
* *The caching daemon is busy **[updating database indexes]**.*  
  (❌ *\*busy at updating / busy to update*).
* *The refactoring effort is worth **[investing time into]**.*
* *The secondary node remained trapped **[waiting for heartbeat signals]**.*

### D. Clausal Complements (Finite Content / $\text{That}$-Clauses)
Selected by adjectives of mental certainty, cognitive evaluation, or emotion (*sure, certain, aware, confident, glad, proud, afraid*):
* *The platform lead was certain **[CP that the hotfix had resolved the leak]**.*
* *The team was confident **[CP that throughput would remain stable]**.*
* *We are aware **[CP that the primary node is unreachable]**.*

* **Role of `that`:** Functions as an optional **Declarative Complementizer ($C^\circ$)** marking the clausal boundary. It leaves no gap and does not replace a noun phrase.

## 3. Structural Boundary Diagnostics: Adjective Complement vs. Adverbial Adjunct

Because both finite content clauses and causal adverbials explain reasons, apply the **Attachment & Subordinator Test**:

| Diagnostic Dimension     | Adjective Complement                                         | Adverbial Adjunct                                            |
| :----------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Syntactic Attachment** | Internal sister to $A^\circ$ inside the **$\text{AP}$ container**. | External clausal modifier adjoined to the **$\text{VP}$ or $\text{TP}$**. |
| **Subordinator Type**    | Declarative Complementizer (*that* / $\emptyset$).           | Circumstantial Subordinating Conjunction (*because, since*). |
| **Semantic Scope**       | Completes the internal lexical definition of the adjective.  | Adds circumstantial causality to the entire matrix event.    |
| **Deletion Impact**      | Loses the propositional argument required by the emotion/state. | Strips background circumstance; core predication unchanged.  |

### Comparative Diagnostic:
* **Adjective Complement:**  
  *"I am **glad** **[CP that you resolved the incident]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *that you resolved the incident* sits inside the $\text{AP}$ headed by *glad*, providing the specific propositional trigger for the mental state.
* **Adverbial Adjunct:**  
  *"He is **happy** **[CP because it is sunny outside]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *because it is sunny outside* is an adverbial clause adjoined to the matrix predicate, detailing an external circumstantial cause.

## 4. Heavy APs and Positional Constraints

When an adjective takes an Adjective Complement (whether a $\text{PP}$, an Infinitival Clause, or a Content $\text{CP}$), the resulting $\text{AP}$ becomes **syntactically heavy**.

Under the **Phrasal Weight Rule**, heavy APs are strictly barred from the pre-nominal attributive position and must shift post-nominally:

* ❌ *\*We hired an [eager to learn Kubernetes] engineer.* (Pre-nominal heavy AP crash).
* ✅ *We hired an engineer **[AP eager to learn Kubernetes]**.* (Postpositive Heavy AP).
* ❌ *\*The team patched a [vulnerable to buffer overflows] microservice.*
* ✅ *The team patched a microservice **[AP vulnerable to buffer overflows]**.*

## 5. Analytical Guardrails & Crash Rules

### Guardrail 1: Tough-Movement Missing Gap Crash
A Tough-Construction **must** contain an open extraction site. Leaving an overt pronoun in the object position crashes the syntax:
* ❌ *\*This legacy code is difficult to read **it**.* (Crash: illegal pronoun retention in extraction gap).
* ✅ *This legacy code$_i$ is difficult to read $\emptyset_i$.* (Valid Tough-Complement).

### Guardrail 2: Adjective Complement vs. Noun Complement
Track the selecting head word class:
* **Noun Complement:** Restricts an abstract noun inside a $\text{DP}$ (*"our certainty [that the node failed]"*).
* **Adjective Complement:** Restricts a predicate or attributive adjective inside an $\text{AP}$ (*"we are certain [that the node failed]"*).

### Guardrail 3: Adjective Complement vs. Purpose Adverbial Clause
Do not confuse an infinitival adjective complement with an event purpose clause:
* *The developer was quick **[to resolve the bug]**.* $\rightarrow$ **Adjective Complement** (Answers: *Quick in what domain / to do what?*).
* *The developer optimized the query **[to resolve the bug]**.* $\rightarrow$ **Adverbial of Purpose** (Can substitute with *in order to*; movable to clause-initial: *"To resolve the bug, the developer optimized..."*).
