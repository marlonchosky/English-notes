# Complex Transitive Verbs (CTV) vs. Transitive Prepositional Verbs

Both verb classes can generate an identical active surface string:
$$\text{[Subject]} + \text{[Verb]} + \text{[Direct Object DP]} + \text{[Prepositional Phrase PP]}$$

However, their underlying syntactic architecture, argument mapping, and complement types diverge fundamentally.

---

## 1. The Core Functional Split

| Dimension              | Complex Transitive Verb (CTV) Frame                          | Transitive Prepositional Verb Frame                          |
| :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Verb Type**          | Complex Transitive Verb ($\text{CTV}$)                       | Transitive Prepositional / Prepositional Ditransitive        |
| **Argument Structure** | $\text{Subj} + \text{V} + \text{DO} + \text{Secondary Predicate / Bound}$ | $\text{Subj} + \text{V} + \text{DO} + \text{Oblique Argument (PP)}$ |
| **PP Function**        | **Adverbial Complement** OR **Object Complement**            | **Prepositional Complement** (Oblique Argument)              |
| **PP Semantic Role**   | Sets an external coordinate, bounded perimeter, or resulting state. | Supplies a required participant/entity slot (Theme, Threat, Co-agent). |
| **Canonical Verbs**    | *put, keep, place, leave, set*                               | *provide ... with, protect ... from, charge ... with*        |

---

## 2. Adverbial Complement vs. Object Complement (Inside CTVs)

Within a Complex Transitive frame, a post-object constituent fulfills one of two distinct structural duties:

```text
Complex Transitive Verb Complements
├── A. Adverbial Complement (Spatial / Bounded Domain):
│      Direct Object is placed at or restricted to an external coordinate/boundary.
│      • "They put the book [PP on the table]."
│      • "Keep the secret [PP to yourself]."
│
└── B. Object Complement (Property, Identity, Result State):
       Direct Object bears an internal attribute, role, or caused state.
       • "They painted the wall [AP yellow]."
       • "The committee elected him [NP manager]."
       • "He hammered the metal [AP flat]."
```

### A. Adverbial Complement (Spatial Coordinates & Bounded Domains)
The complement establishes an **external physical coordinate, directional vector, or bounded perimeter** containing or restricting the direct object. It does not define what the object looks like, nor does it alter the object's intrinsic classification:
* *"They **put** the book **on the table**."*
  * **put:** $\text{CTV}$
  * **the book:** $\text{DO}$
  * **on the table:** Locative Adverbial Complement ($\text{PP}$)
* *"He **kept** the secret **to himself**."*
  * **keep:** $\text{CTV}$
  * **the secret:** $\text{DO}$
  * **to himself:** Adverbial Complement of Bounded Domain ($\text{PP}$)

### B. Object Complement (Internal State, Quality, or Identity)
The complement acts as a **secondary predicate** directly characterizing the Direct Object, assigning it an internal property, mental judgment, resulting state, or nominal identity ($DO \rightarrow State/Identity$):
* *"He painted the door **red**."* (Resultative $\text{AP} \rightarrow$ Object Complement)
* *"They elected her **chairperson**."* (Identity $\text{NP} \rightarrow$ Object Complement)
* *"The board considered the proposal **unviable**."* (Evaluative $\text{AP} \rightarrow$ Object Complement)

## 3. Transitive Prepositional Verbs (Prepositional Ditransitives)

A **Transitive Prepositional Verb** selects two internal arguments: a **Direct Object** and an obligatory **Prepositional Complement** introduced by a fixed, idiosyncratic lexical preposition.

Unlike CTV complements, this $\text{PP}$ is not a secondary predicate and does not locate the direct object in physical space; it introduces a mandatory semantic participant selected directly by the verb root:

* **Sentence:** *"I **provided** him **with the file**."*
  * **provided:** Transitive Prepositional Verb
  * **him:** Direct Object ($\text{DP}$)
  * **with the file:** Prepositional Complement ($\text{PP}$, Oblique Participant)
* **Sentence:** *"The shield **protected** the reactor **from damage**."*
  * **protected:** Transitive Prepositional Verb
  * **the reactor:** Direct Object ($\text{DP}$)
  * **from damage:** Prepositional Complement ($\text{PP}$, Oblique Source/Threat)

> ⚠️ **Syntax Guardrail:** Never label *with the file* or *from damage* as an "Object Complement" or "Adverbial Complement". The phrase does not locate the direct object in physical space, nor does it express an adjective-like result state of the object; it satisfies a subcategorized participant slot demanded by the lexical verb head.

## 4. The Syntactic Fork: The Case of "Leave with"

Certain verbs exhibit structural polysemy, allowing two valid theoretical parses depending on whether the prepositional phrase is interpreted as a secondary state or an oblique transfer argument:

$$\text{"I left him with the bill."}$$

| Analytical Framework        | Complex Transitive (CTV) Lens                                | Transitive Prepositional Lens                                |
| :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Verb Parse**              | **Complex Transitive Verb ($\text{CTV}$)**                   | **Transitive Prepositional Verb**                            |
| **Direct Object**           | *him*                                                        | *him*                                                        |
| **Role of *with the bill*** | **Prepositional Object Complement**                          | **Prepositional Complement** (Oblique Argument)              |
| **Semantic Paraphrase**     | *"I caused [him to be with the bill]"* (Circumstantial state/burden imposed onto the object). | *"I transferred the bill onto him when leaving"* (Three-argument transfer verb frame). |
| **Parallel Construction**   | *I left the server **online**.*                              | *We entrusted him **with the task**.*                        |

Both analyses are linguistically valid:
* Under a **Small-Clause / Secondary Predicate** view, *with the bill* characterizes the resultant state of the object, compiling as a **CTV + Prepositional Object Complement**.
* Under a **Lexical Argument** view, *leave* functions as a 3-place participant verb selecting an Agent, Patient, and Oblique Recipient/Burden, compiling as a **Transitive Prepositional Verb + Prepositional Complement**.
