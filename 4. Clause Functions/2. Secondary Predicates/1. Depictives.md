# Depictives (Depictive Secondary Predicates)

A **Depictive Secondary Predicate** (or simply **Depictive**) characterizes the physical state, psychological condition, social role, or format of a nominal participant (the Subject or the Direct Object) **simultaneously with the occurrence of the main predicate event**.

Unlike a **Resultative** (which denotes an end state *caused by* or *resulting from* the verbal action), a depictive expresses a **concurrent, co-temporal state** ($State\ DURING\ Event$).

---

## 1. Predication Orientation: Subject-Oriented vs. Object-Oriented

A depictive predicate forms an off-branch predication nexus that co-indexes with either the matrix Subject or the Direct Object:

```text
Secondary Depictive Predication
├── A. Subject-Oriented Depictive:
│      Predicates of the Subject concurrent with the action.
│      • "The systems architect reviewed the pull request [AP sober]."
│      • "He delivered the emergency briefing [AP calm]."
│      • "He died [NP a hero]."
│
└── B. Object-Oriented Depictive:
       Predicates of the Direct Object concurrent with the action.
       • "The team consumed the telemetry streams [AP raw]."
       • "She drank the espresso [AP cold]."
       • "We shipped the database backup [AP unencrypted]."
```

### A. Subject-Oriented Depictives
The state describes the matrix Subject throughout the temporal boundary of the event:
* *The developer attended the architecture retro **[AP exhausted]**.*  
  $\rightarrow$ $\text{Condition:}$ The developer was exhausted while attending the retro.
* *The engineer left the operations room **[AP pessimistic]**.*  
  $\rightarrow$ $\text{Condition:}$ The engineer was pessimistic at the moment of leaving.

### B. Object-Oriented Depictives
The state describes the matrix Direct Object at the precise moment it undergoes the verbal event:
* *They deployed the worker nodes **[AP unconfigured]**.*  
  $\rightarrow$ $\text{Condition:}$ The worker nodes were in an unconfigured state during deployment.
* *The DBA ingested the database tables **[AP unpartitioned]**.*  
  $\rightarrow$ $\text{Condition:}$ The tables were unpartitioned when ingested.

---

## 2. Syntactic Status: Depictive Adjuncts vs. Depictive Complements

Depictives distribute across two distinct structural roles based on verb subcategorization:

| Structural Status        | Syntactic Function           | Governing Predicate                                          | The Deletion Diagnostic                                      | Canonical Example                      |
| :----------------------- | :--------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------- |
| **Depictive Adjunct**    | Optional Secondary Predicate | Intransitive, Monotransitive, or Copular frames              | **Passes:** Matrix clause remains complete and semantically intact. | *He drank the coffee **cold**.*        |
| **Depictive Complement** | Obligatory Object Complement | Complex Transitive Verbs ($\text{CTV}$) of judgment or perception | **Fails (Crashes or Alters Frame):** Collapses judgment into physical location. | *They found the harness **unusable**.* |

### A. Depictive Adjuncts (Optional Modifiers)
Depictive adjuncts attach optionally to an already syntactically complete clause. Removing them leaves the core proposition intact:
* *"The senior developer drank the espresso **[AP cold]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *Deletion Test:* *"The senior developer drank the espresso."* ✅ (Fully intact monotransitive proposition).
* *"The technician inspected the bare-metal server **[AP undamaged]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *Deletion Test:* *"The technician inspected the bare-metal server."* ✅ (Intact monotransitive proposition).

### B. Depictive Complements (Obligatory Arguments)
Complex Transitive Verbs of mental evaluation, judgment, or cognitive perception (*find, consider, deem, judge*) subcategorize for an obligatory **Evaluative Depictive Complement** (Object Complement):
* *"The QA team found the legacy test harness **[AP unusable]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *Deletion Test:* *"The QA team found the legacy test harness."* ❌ (Shifts the lexical meaning of *found* from cognitive evaluation to physical location/discovery).
* *"The security auditors consider the cryptographic algorithm **[AP vulnerable]**."*  
  $\rightarrow$ *Deletion Test:* *\*"The security auditors consider the cryptographic algorithm."* ❌ (Syntactic crash: *consider* requires a complement to saturate its argument structure).

## 3. Structural Realization (Category Types)

While depictives are most commonly realized as **Adjective Phrases ($\text{AP}$)**, they can also be projected via **Noun Phrases ($\text{NP}$)** and **Prepositional Phrases ($\text{PP}$)**:

### 1. Adjective Phrase ($\text{AP}$) Realization
* *The on-call engineer joined the incident bridge **[AP furious]**.* (Subject-Oriented Adjunct).
* *We prefer to review raw telemetry logs **[AP uncompressed]**.* (Object-Oriented Adjunct).

### 2. Noun Phrase ($\text{NP}$) Realization
When realized as a noun phrase, the depictive assigns a temporary social identity, role, or status concurrent with the event:
* *He died **[NP a hero]**.* (Subject-Oriented Depictive Adjunct).
* *She entered the organization **[NP an intern]** and left **[NP a principal engineer]**.*

### 3. Prepositional Phrase ($\text{PP}$) Realization
Circumstantial states of attire, format, or condition:
* *The technician entered the server cleanroom **[PP in protective gear]**.*
* *The database backup was archived **[PP in plain text]**.*

---

## 4. Semantic Sub-Types: Physical vs. Evaluative / Mental Depictives

Depictives bifurcate semantically depending on whether the state is an objective material property or a subjective psychological/evaluative judgment:

```text
Semantic Taxonomy
├── Physical Depictive:
│   Objective physical/thermal state, material condition, format, or attire.
│   • "He drank the tea [hot]."
│   • "She ate the salmon [raw]."
│   • "The operator fell asleep [clothed]."
│
└── Evaluative / Mental Depictive:
    Subjective psychological state, emotional disposition, or mental judgment.
    • "The architect proposed the migration [optimistic]."
    • "She joined the retro [furious]."
    • "We consider the pipeline [unreliable]."
```

## 5. Depictive vs. Resultative Boundary Matrix

Never confuse a Depictive (concurrent state) with a Resultative (endpoint state caused by the action):

| Diagnostic Criterion      | Depictive Secondary Predicate                                | Resultative Secondary Predicate                              |
| :------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Temporal Alignment**    | **Concurrent:** State exists *during* the action ($T_{\text{state}} = T_{\text{event}}$). | **Consecutive / Culminating:** State emerges *at the end* of the action ($T_{\text{state}} > T_{\text{event}}$). |
| **Causal Connection**     | **None:** The verb action does *not* cause the state.        | **Direct:** The verb action *causes* the state change.       |
| **Paraphrase Diagnostic** | *"X did Y while X/Y was Z."*                                 | *"X did Y, and as a result, X/Y became Z."*                  |
| **Example 1**             | *He ate the lunch **cold**.* (Lunch was cold while eating).  | *He hammered the metal **flat**.* (Metal became flat from hammering). |
| **Example 2**             | *She wiped the screen **clean**.* ❌ *(Wait: wiped causes screen to become clean $\rightarrow$ Resultative).* | *She wiped the screen **damp**.* (Screen was already damp while wiping $\rightarrow$ Depictive). |

## 6. Analytical Guardrails & Boundary Diagnostics

### Guardrail 1: Depictive Adjective vs. Manner Adverb
A frequent diagnostic error is selecting a manner adverb when the speaker intends to predicate a state of the entity:
* **Depictive Adjective (Entity State):** Answers *"In what state was the entity during the event?"*
  * *The engineer returned to the workstation **calm**.* (Describes the psychological composure of the engineer).
* **Manner Adverb (Action Execution):** Answers *"How was the mechanical verb executed?"*
  * *The engineer returned to the workstation **calmly**.* (Describes the physical manner/stride of walking back).
* ❌ *\*The automated daemon completed its task **silent**.* (Crash: An inanimate background process cannot possess an adjective state of being silent; it requires a manner adverb: *silently*).

### Guardrail 2: The Stative CTV Constraint
Complex Transitive Verbs of cognitive evaluation (*consider, deem, judge*) require **stative depictive complements**. Pairing them with dynamic progressive forms (*being + adjective*) triggers a syntactic crash:
* ✅ *"The board deemed the proposal **unviable**."*
* ❌ *\*"The board deemed the proposal **being unviable**."* (Crash: Dynamic progressive coercion).

### Guardrail 3: Depictive Adjunct vs. Attributive Adjective
Do not confuse an object-oriented depictive adjunct with an attributive pre-nominal adjective:
* **Attributive:** *He drank the **cold** coffee.* (Restricts the reference of the noun; the coffee might have been bought cold).
* **Depictive Secondary Predicate:** *He drank the coffee **cold**.* (Asserts that at the temporal interval of drinking, the coffee was in a cold state).
