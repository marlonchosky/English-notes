# Complements of Verbs by Transitivity

A **Verbal Complement** is an obligatory constituent selected by a verb's subcategorization frame to satisfy its argument structure. Unlike optional adjuncts, omitting a complement results in an ungrammatical clause or fundamentally alters the lexical meaning of the verb.

In formal syntax, verbal complementation patterns are systematically organized by the verb's **Transitivity Frame**:
* **Intransitive Systems (0 Objects):** Pure Intransitives, Complex Intransitive Verbs (CIVs), and Copular/Linking Verbs.
* **Monotransitive Systems (1 Object):** Direct Objects and Prepositional Verbs.
* **Ditransitive Systems (2 Objects):** Double Object Constructions and Prepositional Dative Alternations.
* **Complex Transitive Systems (1 Object + 1 Secondary Predicate):** Direct Objects paired with Object Complements or Adverbial Complements.
* **Measure Complement Systems (Quasi-Objects):** Non-passivizable scalar complements distributed across transitivity frames.

## 1. Intransitive Verb Complementation (0 Objects)

Intransitive verbs do not assign structural accusative case to a direct object. However, they diverge based on whether they require post-verbal constituents to satisfy their predication.

### A. Intransitive Verbs with Verbal Complements
Verbs of agreement, decision, choice, or intention (*agree, decide, hope, arrange*) license non-finite clausal complements:

#### 1. Subject Control Frame ($\text{PRO}_{\text{arb}}$)
The unexpressed subject of the embedded non-finite clause is coreferential with the matrix subject:
* *"The senior architect agreed **to lead the migration**."*
  * **agreed:** Intransitive Verb.
  * **to lead the migration:** Verbal Complement (Non-Finite Infinitival Clause). The unexpressed subject ($\text{PRO}$) is controlled by *the senior architect*.

#### 2. Overt Agent Frame (Complementizer "For" Structure)
Because intransitive verbs cannot assign structural accusative case, introducing an overt lexical subject into the non-finite clause requires the prepositional complementizer **`for`**:
* *"We agreed **for the DevOps team to deploy the hotfix**."*
  * **agreed:** Intransitive Verb.
  * **for:** Functional Complementizer ($C^\circ$) licensing the embedded subject.
  * **the DevOps team:** Lexical Subject of the non-finite clause.
  * **for the DevOps team to deploy the hotfix:** Verbal Complement (Infinitival Clause).

### B. Complex Intransitive Verbs (CIVs)
A Complex Intransitive Verb selects no direct object, but strictly requires an **Adverbial Complement** (Locative, Temporal, or Directional) to bound its action. Deleting this constituent causes an immediate structural crash:
* *"The legacy caching cluster **resides** [PP **in the secondary data center**]."*
  * **resides:** Complex Intransitive Verb (CIV).
  * **in the secondary data center:** Locative Adverbial Complement (PP). *(Deletion crash: \* "The legacy caching cluster resides.")*
* *"The database recovery process **lasted** [PP **until midnight**]."*
  * **lasted:** Complex Intransitive Verb (CIV).
  * **until midnight:** Temporal Adverbial Complement (PP). *(Deletion crash: \* "The recovery process lasted.")*

### C. Copular / Linking Verbs
Copular verbs serve as a relational connector linking the subject to a **Subject Complement ($\text{SC}$)** that attributes an identity, state, or property to that subject:
* **Static Linking Verbs (State/Identity):**
  * *"The orchestration framework **remained** [AP **operational**] throughout the failover."*
  * *"The senior engineer **became** [NP **an infrastructure architect**]."*
* **Dynamic / Aspectual Linking Verbs (Change of State):**
  * *"The staging proxy **went** [AP **unresponsive**] after the memory spike."* (Movement away from ideal state).
  * *"Our deployment hypothesis **came** [AP **true**] under production load."* (Movement toward ideal state).

## 2. Monotransitive Verb Complementation (1 Object)

Monotransitive verbs select a single internal argument to satisfy their argument structure.

### A. Direct Object Monotransitives ($S + V + DO$)
Selects a single nominal phrase ($\text{DP/NP}$) and assigns structural Accusative Case:
* *"The site reliability team fixed [DP **the memory leak**]."*
* *"The deployment pipeline requires [DP **immediate manual verification**]."*

### B. Prepositional Verbs ($S + V + \text{Prepositional Complement}$)
Intransitive or semi-lexical verbs that subcategorize for a fixed, idiosyncratic prepositional "hook." The preposition is structurally selected by the verb root, and the following nominal is a **Prepositional Complement** (Oblique Argument), not an optional adjunct:
* *"The orchestrator listens [PP **for incoming health probe signals**]."*
  * **listens:** Prepositional Verb.
  * **for incoming health probe signals:** Prepositional Complement (PP).
* *"The database administrator listened [PP **to the incident recording**]."*
  * **listened:** Prepositional Verb.
  * **to the incident recording:** Prepositional Complement (PP).

---

## 3. Ditransitive Verb Complementation (2 Objects)

Ditransitive verbs select two internal arguments: a Recipient/Goal and a Theme/Patient. They participate in the **Dative Alternation**.

### A. Double Object Construction ($S + V + IO + DO$)
Both arguments are realized as nominal phrases ($\text{DPs}$):
* *"The platform team granted [DP **the developer**] [DP **administrative privileges**]."*
  * **granted:** Ditransitive Verb.
  * **the developer:** Indirect Object (IO, Recipient).
  * **administrative privileges:** Direct Object (DO, Theme).

### B. Prepositional Dative Alternation ($S + V + DO + \text{Prepositional Complement}$)
The Theme surfaces as a direct object, while the Goal/Recipient surfaces inside a prepositional phrase headed by *to* or *for*:
* *"The security engineer delivered [DP **the encrypted key**] [PP **to the security officer**]."*
  * **delivered:** Ditransitive Verb.
  * **the encrypted key:** Direct Object (DO).
  * **to the security officer:** Prepositional Complement (PP).

## 4. Complex Transitive Verb Complementation (CTVs)

$$\mathbf{\text{Subject}} + \mathbf{\text{CTV}} + \mathbf{\text{Direct Object (DO)}} + \mathbf{\text{Secondary Complement}}$$

A **Complex Transitive Verb (CTV)** selects a Direct Object and an obligatory secondary predicate that characterizes, defines, locates, or establishes the resultant state of that object.

### A. Object Complements (State, Quality, Role, or Result)
Modifies the internal features or condition of the direct object:
* **Depictive AP (Mental Evaluation / Judgment):**
  * *"The security team found [DP **the root certificate**] [AP **invalid**]."*
  * *"The operations team considers [DP **the new build**] [AP **unstable**]."*
* **Resultative AP (Terminal State Change):**
  * *"The maintenance script hammered [DP **the compressed tables**] [AP **flat**]."*
  * *"They painted [DP **the server chassis**] [AP **matte black**]."*
* **Noun Phrase Identity (Role / Title):**
  * *"The product team elected [DP **her**] [NP **scrum master**]."*
* **Bare Infinitive Phrase (Causatives & Perception Verbs):**
  * *"The tech lead made [DP **the developer**] [VP **review the pull request again**]."*
  * *"The site reliability team watched [DP **the CPU usage**] [VP **spike to 100%**]."*
* **To-Infinitive Phrase (Catenatives / Persuasion):**
  * *"The platform lead got [DP **the junior developer**] [TP **to configure the ingress controller**]."*
* **Present Participle Phrase (Ongoing Real-Time Event):**
  * *"The operations engineer heard [DP **the disk arrays**] [VP **failing intermittently**]."*
* **Past Participle Phrase (Passive Result State):**
  * *"The platform team had [DP **the staging database**] [VP **restored from snapshot**]."*

### B. Adverbial Complements (External Spatial / Locative Coordinates)
Unlike Object Complements (which define the object's intrinsic state), an Adverbial Complement places the direct object into an external physical or bounded coordinate. Omitting the coordinate crashes the verb frame:
* *"We placed [DP **the configuration file**] [PP **on the production server**]."*
  * **placed:** Complex Transitive Verb (CTV).
  * **the configuration file:** Direct Object (DO).
  * **on the production server:** Locative Adverbial Complement (PP). *(Crash: \* "We placed the configuration file.")*
* *"They put [DP **the binary**] [PP **into the root directory**]."*

## 5. The Measure Complement System (Quasi-Objects)

A **Measure Complement** (or *Quasi-Object*) is a nominal phrase ($\text{NP/DP}$) that quantifies scalar properties such as temporal duration, distance, cost, weight, or volume. 

Although measure complements occupy nominal argument positions, they **fail the Passive Promotion Transformation Test**:
* *"The server reboot took forty-five minutes."*  
  $\rightarrow$ ❌ *\* "Forty-five minutes was taken by the server reboot."* (Crash: Measure phrases cannot be promoted to passive subjects).

Measure complements are classified across four distinct transitivity groups:

| Group       | Transitivity Frame                        | Structural Formula                                | Syntactic Mechanism                                          | Technical Example                                            |
| :---------- | :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Group 1** | **Intransitive Measure Complement (CIV)** | $S + V_{\text{CIV}} + \text{Measure NP}$          | Scalar intransitive verb where the subject undergoes the measurement. | *"The cloud instance **costs five hundred dollars a month**."*<br>*"The server rack **weighs two hundred pounds**."* |
| **Group 2** | **Transitive Measure Complement**         | $S + V_{\text{trans}} + \text{Measure NP}$        | Monotransitive verb of consumption or duration taking a measure phrase in the direct object slot. | *"The cluster failover drill **took forty-five minutes**."*<br>*"The database indexing run **took six hours**."* |
| **Group 3** | **Ditransitive Measure Complement**       | $S + V_{\text{ditrans}} + IO + \text{Measure NP}$ | Ditransitive frame where the indirect object represents the entity experiencing or paying the measurement. | *"The cloud provider billed **our team two thousand dollars**."*<br>*"The migration cost **us twelve hours of downtime**."* |
| **Group 4** | **Prepositional Measure Complement**      | $S + V_{\text{trans}} + DO + PP_{\text{measure}}$ | Monotransitive verb taking a direct object followed by an obligatory scalar prepositional phrase. | *"The engineer timed **the indexing job at three hours**."*<br>*"We clocked **the throughput at ten gigabits per second**."* |

## 7. Syntactic Crash Diagnostics

### Diagnostic 1: Passive Promotion of Measure Complements
* ❌ *\*"Three hours **was taken** by the database migration."*
* **Root Cause:** *Three hours* is a Transitive Measure Complement (Quasi-Object) measuring temporal consumption, not a referential Direct Object. It fails the passive transformation test.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The database migration **took three hours**."*

### Diagnostic 2: Missing Complementizer "For" with Overt Non-Finite Subjects
* ❌ *\*"The lead architect agreed **him to merge** the feature branch."*
* **Root Cause:** Intransitive verbs of agreement (*agree, decide, arrange*) cannot assign structural accusative case to an embedded overt agent. An explicit complementizer (*for*) is syntactically required.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The lead architect agreed **for him to merge** the feature branch."*

### Diagnostic 3: Causative Complementation Selection Violation
* ❌ *\*"The engineering director made the team **to work** over the weekend."*
* **Root Cause:** In the active voice, the causative verb *make* strictly selects a Bare Infinitive Complement, rejecting the *to*-infinitive marker.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The engineering director made the team **work** over the weekend."*

### Diagnostic 4: Aspectual Verb Directional Collision (*Come* vs. *Go*)
* ❌ *\*"After the power interruption, our automated failover routines **came wrong**."*
* **Root Cause:** The dynamic linking verb *come* selects transitions toward an ideal or positive state (*come true, come right*). Transitions toward failure, decay, or system deterioration strictly select *go*.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"After the power interruption, our automated failover routines **went wrong**."*

### Diagnostic 5: CIV Spatial Complement Omission Crash
* ❌ *\*"The site reliability engineer placed the configuration manifest."*
* **Root Cause:** *Place* is a Complex Transitive Verb subcategorizing for both a Direct Object and an obligatory Locative Adverbial Complement. Omitting the spatial coordinate leaves the verb frame unsaturated.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The site reliability engineer placed the configuration manifest **into the production repository**."*
