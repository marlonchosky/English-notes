# Complementation Patterns of Agree: Clausal Selection, Control, and Constraints

The verb **`agree`** is an intransitive verb of consent, volition, and propositional alignment. While it cannot assign structural Accusative Case to an object, it is syntactically versatile, selecting across **five distinct complement types** while enforcing strict selectional bans on others.

---

## 1. The Complementation Inventory of `agree`

```text
The Clausal & Phrasal Complements of AGREE
│
├── 1. Finite Declarative Content Clause (Indicative CP)
│      └── [CP that [TP ... modal / past tense ...]]
│
├── 2. Mandative Subjunctive Clause (Base-Form CP)
│      └── [CP that [TP ... bare infinitive V° ...]]
│
├── 3. Subject-Controlled Non-Finite Clause (PRO-Control TP)
│      └── [TP PRO_i to [VP ...]]
│
├── 4. Infinitival Clause with Overt Subject (Prepositional Complementizer CP)
│      └── [CP for [TP DP_overt to [VP ...]]]
│
└── 5. Prepositional Complements (PP Frame)
       └── [PP to / on / with [DP ...]]
```

## 2. Structural Deep Dive by Complementation Type

### 1. Finite Declarative Content Clause (Indicative $\text{CP}$)
* **Example:** *"He agreed **[CP that his father should go to the meeting]**."*
* **Example (Tech Context):** *"The developer agreed **[CP that the schema was deprecated]**."*
* **X-Bar Blueprint:**
  $$\text{[}_{\text{VP}}\ \text{[}_{\text{V}^\circ}\ \text{agreed]}\ \text{[}_{\text{CP}}\ \text{[}_{\text{C}^\circ}\ \text{that]}\ \text{[}_{\text{TP}}\ \text{DP}\ \text{[}_{\text{T}^\circ}\ \text{should / [+Past]]}\ \text{[}_{\text{VP}}\dots]]]]]$$
* **Syntactic Mechanics:**
  * The verb $V^\circ$ selects an entire finite declarative **$\text{CP}$** complement.
  * $C^\circ$ is occupied by the declarative complementizer **`that`**.
  * The embedded subject (`his father` / `the schema`) receives **Nominative Case** internally from the finite $T^\circ$ (`should` / `[+Past]`).

---

### 2. Mandative Subjunctive Clause (Finite $\text{CP}$ with Bare $V^\circ$)
* **Example:** *"He agreed **[CP that his father go to the meeting]**."*
* **Example (Tech Context):** *"The team agreed **[CP that the production cluster be restarted]**."*
* **Syntactic Mechanics:**
  * Introduces an uninflected base verb (`go`, `be`) in the embedded clause without modal auxiliaries.
  * $C^\circ$ hosts `that`, but embedded $T^\circ$ lacks finite tense/agreement features, licensing the bare root verb in the lower predicate.

---

### 3. Subject-Controlled Non-Finite Clause ($\text{PRO}$-Control $\text{TP}$)
* **Example:** *"$\text{He}_i$ agreed **[TP $\text{PRO}_i$ to go to the meeting]**."*
* **Example (Tech Context):** *"$\text{The architect}_i$ agreed **[TP $\text{PRO}_i$ to review the pull request]**."*
* **Syntactic Mechanics:**
  * The matrix subject is the semantic **Agent** performing the embedded action.
  * The subject position of the non-finite clause ($\text{Spec, TP}$) is occupied by the covert pronoun **$\mathbf{\text{PRO}_i}$**, which is **obligatorily bound and co-indexed** with the matrix subject.
  * $T^\circ$ contains non-finite `to`, which assigns **Null Case** to $\text{PRO}$.

---

### 4. Infinitival Clause with Overt Subject ($C^\circ$ `for`)
* **Example:** *"He agreed **[CP for his father to go to the meeting]**."*
* **Example (Tech Context):** *"The lead agreed **[CP for the DevOps team to patch the node]**."*
* **X-Bar Blueprint:**
```text
                     VP (Matrix Predicate)
                    /  \
                  V°    CP (Embedded Clausal Complement)
                agreed /  \
                    Spec   C'
                     Ø    /  \
                         C°   TP (Non-Finite Core)
                        for  /  \
                          Spec    T'
                           |     /  \
                      [DP father] T°   VP
                                  to  /  \
                                     V°   PP
                                     go  [to the meeting]
```
* **Syntactic Mechanics & Case Licensing:**
  * Because *agree* is an intransitive verb, it **cannot assign Accusative Case across clausal boundaries** (unlike ECM verbs such as *want*, *expect*, or *believe*).
  * To prevent a **Case Filter crash**, English inserts the prepositional complementizer **`for`** into the $C^\circ$ head.
  * $C^\circ$ (`for`) assigns structural **Accusative Case** to the overt embedded subject (`his father` / `him`) under sisterhood/c-command.

---

### 5. Prepositional Complements ($\text{PP}$ / Prepositional Verb Frame)
When taking non-clausal nominal complements, *agree* functions as a **Prepositional Verb**, requiring fixed functional prepositional heads:
* **`agree to` (Proposals, terms, conditions):** *"They agreed **[PP to the proposed SLA]**."*
* **`agree on` / `upon` (Joint decisions, shared topics):** *"The teams agreed **[PP on a unified schema]**."*
* **`agree with` (Consenting with a person or idea):** *"The senior dev agreed **[PP with the diagnosis]**."*

## 3. The Core Selectional Constraints

### Constraint 1: The Gerund Prohibition (Infinitive-Only Class)
Unlike verbs that tolerate or require gerundive complements (*enjoy*, *avoid*, *consider*), **`agree` strictly rejects gerunds** when describing actions:
* ❌ *\*"He agreed going to the meeting."* (Severe syntactic crash).
* ❌ *\*"He agreed to going to the meeting."* (Ungrammatical: *to* here is an infinitival marker, not an aspectual preposition accepting a gerund phrase).
* ✅ *"He agreed **to go** to the meeting."*

> 💡 **Notebook Rule:** Along with *decide, refuse, promise,* and *hope*, *agree* belongs to the strict **Infinitive-Only Volitional Verb Class**.

### Constraint 2: The Direct Object Prohibition (Intransitivity)
Because *agree* is strictly intransitive in modern standard English, it **cannot assign structural Accusative Case directly to a noun phrase**:
* ❌ *\*"He agreed the plan."* (Non-standard / ungrammatical in standard American English; requires a preposition: *"agreed **to** the plan"*).
* ❌ *\*"He agreed him to leave."* (Cannot license ECM accusative subjects without the complementizer *for*).

## 4. Master Diagnostic Matrix: `agree` vs. `want`

| Structural Frame                                 |            `agree`             |          `want`          | Notes / Syntactic Reason                                     |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------: | :----------------------: | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Declarative Content Clause (`that`-clause)**   |      ✅ *"agreed that..."*      |  ❌ *\*"wants that..."*   | *want* rejects finite $\text{CP}$ complements entirely.      |
| **Mandative Subjunctive Clause**                 |    ✅ *"agreed that he go"*     | ❌ *\*"wants that he go"* | *agree* licenses subjunctive propositional frames.           |
| **Subject Control ($\text{PRO}$ to-infinitive)** |       ✅ *"agreed to go"*       |    ✅ *"wants to go"*     | Both license $\text{PRO}$ co-indexed with matrix subject.    |
| **Infinitival Clause with Overt Subject**        | ✅ *"agreed **for** him to go"* |  ✅ *"wants him to go"*   | *want* is ECM (licenses accusative *him* directly); *agree* requires $C^\circ$ *for*. |
| **Gerund Complement (`-ing`)**                   |      ❌ *\*"agreed going"*      |   ❌ *\*"wants going"*    | Both verbs strictly ban gerund clausal complements.          |
