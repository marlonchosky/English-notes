# Accusative Verbs vs. Unaccusative Verbs: Argument Mapping and Resultative Licensing

In formal generative syntax, the fundamental division between an **Accusative Verb** and an **Unaccusative Verb** is governed by **Deep Argument Structure** and **Case Assignment** (Burzio's Generalization).

* **Accusative Verbs:** External arguments (Agents) project into subject position ($\text{Spec, TP}$), and the verb assigns structural Accusative Case to an internal Direct Object argument ($\text{DP}$).
* **Unaccusative Verbs:** The verb lacks an external argument (Agent) and cannot assign accusative case. The sole argument is an underlying internal **Patient or Theme** that must raise out of the Verb Phrase into subject position ($\text{Spec, TP}$) to satisfy the Extended Projection Principle (EPP).

---

## 1. Master Structural Comparison

| Feature                          | Accusative Verb (Transitive Frame)                           | Unaccusative Verb (Patient-Subject Intransitive)             |
| :------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Syntactic Blueprint**          | $\text{Subj (Agent)} + V_{\text{trans}} + \text{Direct Object (Patient)}$ | $\text{Subj (Patient/Theme)}_i + V_{\text{intrans}} + t_i$   |
| **Accusative Case Assigned?**    | ✅ **YES** (Assigned to post-verbal Direct Object)            | ❌ **NO** (Verb is *un-accusative*; no accusative case is licensed) |
| **Thematic Role of Subject**     | **Agent / Initiator / Causer**                               | **Patient / Theme / Undergoer**                              |
| **Underlying Origin of Subject** | Base-generated externally in $\text{Spec, } v\text{P}$       | Base-generated internally as the complement of $V^\circ$     |
| **Passivization Capability**     | ✅ Freely passivizes ($\text{DO} \longrightarrow \text{Passive Subject}$) | ❌ Blocked (Underlying object has already raised to subject)  |
| **Baseline Example**             | *"The systems engineer **rebooted** the server."*            | *"The server **rebooted** automatically."*                   |

## 2. Core Mechanics: Why "Un-accusative"?

Under standard generative syntax, verbs that assign structural accusative case to an object require an external agent in their specifier position.

```text
Deep Structure Argument Mapping
├── A. Accusative Structure:
│      [vP The engineer (Agent) [V' rebooted [DP the server (Patient)]]]
│      • "The engineer" raises to Spec, TP (Subject).
│      • "the server" stays in VP complement position and receives Accusative Case.
│
└── B. Unaccusative Structure:
       [vP Ø [V' rebooted [DP the server (Patient)]]]
       • No external agent exists in Spec, vP.
       • Verb cannot assign Accusative Case to "the server".
       • "the server" MUST raise into Spec, TP to receive Nominative Case:
         "[DP The server]_i rebooted t_i"
```

Because the underlying patient entity is promoted to the grammatical subject position, leaving no post-verbal direct object slot to receive accusative case, the construction is termed **Unaccusative**.

## 3. The 3-Way Taxonomy of Unaccusatives & Resultatives

Because the surface subject of an unaccusative verb is an underlying patient/theme, unaccusatives naturally license **Resultative Secondary Predicates** that specify the final state or physical culmination of that subject.

Unaccusative predicates split into three distinct structural behaviors:

```text
Unaccusative Verbs (Patient-Subject Intransitives)
│
├── 1. Bare / Standalone Unaccusatives (Complete Intransitive Events)
│      • Lexically telic; event is syntactically and semantically closed with zero secondary predicates.
│      • Examples: "The butter melted." / "The build crashed." / "The train arrived."
│
├── 2. Standalone Unaccusatives + Optional Resultative Adjuncts
│      • Base clause is fully grammatical and saturated on its own.
│      • Added secondary predicate is grammatically optional (an Adjunct) emphasizing the final boundary.
│      • Examples: "The river froze [solid]." / "The cache container melted [flat]."
│
└── 3. Complex Unaccusatives + Obligatory Resultative Complements
       • The verb denotes an open physical trajectory or radical phase change that crashes without an endpoint.
       • Secondary predicate is syntactically mandatory (a Complement).
       ├── AP Subject Complement: "The door blew open." / "The gate swung open."
       └── PP Resultative Complement: "The vase broke into pieces." / "The cluster split into shards."
```

### Group 1: Bare / Standalone Unaccusatives (Zero Secondary Predicate)
Robust unaccusatives inherently contain an intrinsic event boundary (culmination) within their lexical root:
* *"The redundant backup disk **failed**."*
* *"The ice **melted**."*
* *"The packet queue **cleared**."*
* **Syntactic Status:** Saturated and complete as bare intransitive clauses ($S + V$).

### Group 2: Standalone Unaccusatives with Optional Resultative Adjuncts
When a standalone unaccusative is already complete, an extra Adjective Phrase ($\text{AP}$) can be attached to emphasize the absolute final state. Because deleting the adjective leaves a fully grammatical sentence, it functions as a **Resultative Adjunct**:
* *"The coolant conduit **froze** [**solid**]."*
  * **Base Clause:** *"The coolant conduit froze."* (Complete, fully grammatical clause).
  * **Syntactic Role:** *solid* is an optional **Subject Resultative Adjunct**.
* *"The staging storage partition **melted** [**flat**]."*
  * **Base Clause:** *"The staging storage partition melted."* (Complete sentence).
  * **Syntactic Role:** *flat* is an optional **Subject Resultative Adjunct**.

### Group 3: Complex Unaccusatives with Obligatory Resultative Complements
Certain unaccusative verbs express an open kinetic trajectory or denote a radical physical phase change that requires a structural delimiter to close the event. Deleting the secondary predicate causes the clause to crash or lose its intended bounded sense:

1. **Adjective Phrase ($\text{AP}$) Subject Complements (Kinetic Trajectory Delimiters):**
   * *"The emergency release hatch **blew** **open**."*  
     $\rightarrow$ Air pressure/force moves the hatch. Deleting *open* leaves *\*"The hatch blew"* semantically incomplete.  
     $\rightarrow$ **Syntactic Role:** Obligatory **Resultative Subject Complement**.
   * *"The isolation valve **swung** **shut**."*  
     $\rightarrow$ **Syntactic Role:** Obligatory **Resultative Subject Complement**.
2. **Prepositional Phrase ($\text{PP}$) Complements (The "Into" Transition Rule):**
   * *"The optical fiber cable **snapped** **into pieces**."*
   * *"The volatile memory cache **dissolved** **into garbage data**."*
   * **The "Into" Rule:** When an unaccusative denotes radical physical transformation, fragmentation, or phase change, English selects a $\text{PP}$ headed by *into*. Because the verb requires this trajectory to mark event culmination, the $\text{PP}$ is an obligatory **Prepositional Resultative Complement** (not an optional adjunct).

## 4. Theoretical Guardrail: Complex Unaccusatives vs. Dynamic Linking Verbs

Both constructions require an obligatory secondary predicate to complete an intransitive state change, but their underlying verbal nature diverges:

```text
                  Obligatory Intransitive Transitions
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                   ▼
Complex Unaccusative                                Dynamic Linking Verb
(Lexical Motion / Trajectory)                       (Pure Aspectual Vector)
• Lexical verb with physical kinetics               • Functional verb acting as an arrow (→)
• Adjective represents a SPATIAL DELIMITER          • Adjective represents a QUALITATIVE STATE
• Alternates into a Transitive Causative (Ergative) • Rejects Causative Alternation (Non-Ergative)
  ("The wind blew the door open")                     (*"The heat became the water salty")
```

### Granular Distinctions
1. **Physical Kinetics vs. Relational Vector:**
   * In *"The door blew open"*, *blew* describes real physical air pressure moving the door.
   * In *"The milk turned sour"*, *turned* has no physical rotational meaning; it is purely an aspectual vector ($\text{Subject} \rightarrow \text{State}$).
2. **Causative / Ergative Alternation Test:**
   * Complex unaccusatives alternate symmetrically into a transitive causative frame:  
     *"The pressure **blew the door open**."* (Valid Complex Transitive Verb frame).
   * Dynamic linking verbs reject the causative alternation:  
     ❌ *\*"The ambient heat **turned the milk sour**."* *(Note: while colloquial English permits this with 'turn', copular 'become' and 'go' trigger total structural crashes: \*"The heat went the milk sour", \*"The cook became the water salty").*
3. **Category Selection ($c$-selection for Nominal Identity):**
   * Dynamic copular verbs license direct Noun Phrases as identity complements (*"She became **a systems architect**"*).
   * Unaccusatives cannot license bare identity nominals (❌ *\*"The ice melted water"* $\rightarrow$ requires prepositional path: *"The ice melted **into water**"*).

## 6. Syntactic Crash Diagnostics

### Diagnostic 1: The Evaluative Adjective Prohibition
* ❌ *\*"The database server crashed **terrible** during the traffic spike."*
* **Root Cause:** Unaccusative verbs of physical failure or state change reject subjective evaluative adjectives (*terrible, magnificent, beautiful*) as resultatives. Evaluative adjectives denote speaker judgment rather than an objective physical culmination.
* ✅ **Fix (Manner Adverbial Adjunct):** *"The database server crashed **terribly** during the traffic spike."*
* ✅ **Fix (Physical Resultative PP):** *"The database server crashed **into an unrecoverable state**."*

### Diagnostic 2: Dropped Preposition in Radical Transformations
* ❌ *\*"The corrupted hard drive shattered **fine powder** under the industrial press."*
* **Root Cause:** Unaccusative verbs cannot assign accusative case or directly select bare nominal phrases to express transformed identities. Radical material transitions strictly demand an *into*-prepositional complement.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The corrupted hard drive shattered **into fine powder** under the industrial press."*

### Diagnostic 3: Non-Alternating Transitive Inchoative Crash
* ❌ *\*"The automated script terminated the background session."* $\longrightarrow$ *\*"The background session **terminated**."*
* **Root Cause:** Pure agentive transitive verbs (such as *terminate*, *destroy*, *demolish*) do not possess an intransitive unaccusative twin; they strictly require an external agent. Converting them to a subject-undergoer reading requires morphological **Passive Voice Marking**.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The background session **was terminated** by the automated script."*
