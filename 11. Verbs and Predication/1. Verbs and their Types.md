# Verbs and Their Types: Transitivity, Valency, and Semantic Classes

A **Verb ($V^\circ$)** is the structural nucleus of the clause. Within generative syntax, a verb's lexical entry encodes its **Argument Structure** (the number and type of participants it selects) and its **Subcategorization Frame** (the syntactic categories it licenses as complements).

Verbs are classified across two complementary lenses:
1. **Syntactic Valency & Transitivity:** How many and what types of arguments the verb selects (Intransitive, Monotransitive, Ditransitive, Complex Transitive, Copular).
2. **Semantic / Aspectual Classes:** The semantic nature of the event (State, Motion, Perception, Causation, Emission, Change of State).

---

## 1. Master Valency & Transitivity Taxonomy

Syntactic transitivity measures the number of obligatory non-subject arguments selected by the verbal head ($V^\circ$):

```text
The Verbal Transitivity Hierarchy
├── 0 Objects (Intransitive Systems):
│   ├── Pure Intransitive: No complements (S + V)
│   ├── Complex Intransitive Verb (CIV): Mandatory Adverbial or Measure Complement (S + CIV + Complement)
│   └── Copular / Linking: Mandatory Subject Complement (S + Copula + SC)
│
├── 1 Object (Monotransitive Systems):
│   ├── Direct Object Monotransitive: Selects an accusative DP (S + V + DO)
│   └── Prepositional Verb: Selects a bound prepositional hook (S + V + [P° + DP])
│
├── 2 Objects (Ditransitive Systems):
│   ├── Double Object Frame: Indirect Object + Direct Object (S + V + IO + DO)
│   └── Prepositional Dative Frame: Direct Object + Prepositional Complement (S + V + DO + [P° + DP])
│
└── 1 Object + Secondary Predicate (Complex Transitive Systems):
    └── Complex Transitive Verb (CTV): Direct Object + Object/Adverbial Complement (S + CTV + DO + OC/AC)
```

## 2. Intransitive Verbs: Sub-Classes and Complementation

Intransitive verbs do not assign structural accusative case to an independent object noun phrase. However, they diverge based on whether they require post-verbal constituents to complete their predication:

### A. Pure Intransitive Verbs
Require zero complements; the event is syntactically and semantically closed with the subject alone.
* *"The caching daemon **restarted**."*
* *"The senior developer **laughed**."*

### B. Complex Intransitive Verbs (CIV)
Intransitive verbs that cannot stand alone because they require an **Adverbial Complement** (Locative/Directional/Temporal) or a **Measure Complement**:
* **Locative Coordinate Required:** *"The database cluster **resides** [PP in the secondary data center]."* (Dropping the PP crashes the frame: *\*The cluster resides*).
* **Temporal / Duration Required:** *"The backup migration **lasted** [DP three hours]."*
* **Measure / Scale Required:** *"The rack hardware **weighs** [DP fifty pounds]."* / *"The compute instance **costs** [DP two hundred dollars]."*

### C. Copular / Linking Verbs
Verbs that serve as an equals sign ($=$) or transitional vector arrow ($\rightarrow$) connecting the subject to a **Subject Complement ($\text{SC}$)** (an $NP$ or $AP$) that attributes an identity or quality to that subject:
* **Static Linking Verbs (State/Identity):** *be, remain, seem, appear, look, sound*.
  * *"The deployment pipeline **is** [AP operational]."*
  * *"The architecture **remained** [AP resilient] under load."*
* **Dynamic Linking Verbs (Aspectual Change of State):** *become, turn, grow, go, come*.
  * *"The node **became** [NP a primary replica]."*
  * *"The milk **went** [AP sour]."* (Negative/deteriorative change of state).
  * *"Our predictions **came** [AP true]."* (Positive fulfillment toward an ideal state).

## 3. Transitive and Ditransitive Systems

### A. Monotransitive Verbs ($S + V + DO$)
Select a single internal argument to which they assign structural Accusative Case:
* *"The script **patched** [DP the vulnerability]."*
* *"The orchestrator **allocated** [DP additional memory]."*

### B. Prepositional Verbs ($S + V + \text{Prepositional Complement}$)
Intransitive or transitive verbs that subcategorize for a fixed, idiosyncratic prepositional hook:
* *"The ingress proxy **listens** [PP for incoming connections]."*
* *"We **relied** [PP on the automated failover]."*

### C. Ditransitive Verbs ($S + V + IO + DO$)
Select two internal arguments (Goal/Recipient and Theme/Patient). They exhibit the **Dative Alternation**:
* **Double Object Construction:** *"The architect **granted** [DP the engineer] [DP root access]."*
* **Prepositional Dative Alternation:** *"The architect **granted** [DP root access] [PP to the engineer]."*

---

## 4. Complex Transitive Verbs (CTVs)

**Structural Formula:** $\mathbf{[\text{Subject}] + [\text{CTV}] + [\text{Direct Object}] + [\text{Secondary Complement}]}$

A Complex Transitive Verb selects a Direct Object and a secondary predicate that characterizes, locates, or bounds that direct object:

| CTV Secondary Complement Type          | Form Realization        | Technical Example                                            |
| :------------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Object Complement: Depictive AP**    | Adjective Phrase        | *"We found [DP the database cluster] [AP **unstable**]."*    |
| **Object Complement: Resultative AP**  | Adjective Phrase        | *"The script hammered [DP the buffer] [AP **flat**]."*       |
| **Object Complement: NP Identity**     | Noun Phrase             | *"Management appointed [DP her] [NP **lead architect**]."*   |
| **Object Complement: Bare Infinitive** | Non-Finite $VP$         | *"The lead made [DP the team] [VP **restart the proxy**]."*  |
| **Object Complement: To-Infinitive**   | Infinitival Clause      | *"Security forced [DP us] [TP **to rotate the keys**]."*     |
| **Object Complement: Participle**      | Present/Past Participle | *"We watched [DP the metrics] [VP **spiking**]."* / *"We had [DP the disk] [VP **restored**]."* |
| **Adverbial Complement: Locative PP**  | Prepositional Phrase    | *"The engineer put [DP the binary] [PP **into the root path**]."* |

## 5. Lexical & Semantic Verb Classes

Verbs are also grouped by the semantic nature of their predication, which dictates their complementation patterns:

### 1. Verbs of Perception (*see, watch, hear, feel, notice*)
Function as Complex Transitive Verbs selecting a Direct Object and an aspectual non-finite clause:
* **Bare Infinitive (Completed Event from Start to Finish):** *"We watched the process **terminate**."*
* **Present Participle (Ongoing, Incomplete Event in Progress):** *"We watched the process **terminating**."*
* **Past Participle (Passive Result State):** *"We saw the vulnerability **patched**."*

### 2. Causative Verbs (*make, have, get, let*)
Express the initiation of an event by an agent through an intermediate participant:
* *Make* selects a **Bare Infinitive**: *"They made the daemon **restart**."* (Not: *\*made to restart*).
* *Get* selects a **To-Infinitive**: *"They got the daemon **to restart**."*
* *Have* selects a **Bare Infinitive** (Active) or **Past Participle** (Passive Result): *"We had the vendor **audit** the code."* / *"We had the code **audited**."*

### 3. Aspectual / Directional Linking Verbs (*Go* vs. *Come*)
Dynamic linking verbs carry directional semantic constraints relative to an ideal state:
* **`Go` + Adjective (Movement AWAY from the ideal):** Encodes deterioration, decay, or failure (*go bad, go wrong, go sour, go offline*).
* **`Come` + Adjective (Movement TOWARD the ideal):** Encodes positive realization, accuracy, or fulfillment (*come true, come right*).

### 4. Verbs of Emission (*sparkle, glow, shine, smell, buzz*)
Intransitive predicates that denote the outward projection of light, sound, or substance:
* *"The status LED **blinks** continuously under load."*

### 5. Verbs of Residence & Spatial Stasis (*reside, dwell, live, sit*)
Complex Intransitive Verbs requiring an obligatory locative spatial coordinate:
* *"The encryption keys **reside** inside the hardware security module."*

## 6. Syntactic Crash Diagnostics

### Diagnostic 1: CIV Complement Omission Crash
* ❌ *\*"The database administrator placed the backup snapshot."*
* **Root Cause:** *Place* is a Complex Transitive Verb subcategorizing for both a Direct Object and an obligatory Locative Adverbial Complement. Omitting the spatial coordinate crashes the frame.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The database administrator placed the backup snapshot **on the cold-storage server**."*

### Diagnostic 2: Causative Complementation Selection Violation
* ❌ *\*"The incident commander made the team **to roll back** the release."*
* **Root Cause:** In active voice, causative *make* strictly selects a bare infinitive phrase, filtering out the infinitival complementizer *to*.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The incident commander made the team **roll back** the release."*

### Diagnostic 3: Aspectual Vector Direction Collision (*Come* vs. *Go*)
* ❌ *\*"After the DNS configuration failed, our performance benchmarks **came wrong**."*
* **Root Cause:** Movement toward failure or system degradation requires the deteriorative aspectual vector *go*; *come* is restricted to transitions toward an ideal state.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"After the DNS configuration failed, our performance benchmarks **went wrong**."*

### Diagnostic 4: Passive Promotion of Measure Complements
* ❌ *\*"Three hours **was taken** by the migration script."*
* **Root Cause:** Although *take* occupies a transitive frame, *three hours* functions as a Measure Complement (quasi-object) rather than a referential Direct Object. It cannot undergo A-movement to subject position in a passive clause.
* ✅ **Fix:** *"The migration script **took three hours**."*
